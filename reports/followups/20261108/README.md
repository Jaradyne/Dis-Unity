The next scheduled forecast is November 10. The often-cited roughly $4.49 figure is the **whole-2027 average**, not January's forecast. The [quarterly table](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/tables/pdf/2tab.pdf) separately lists $4.92 for Q1 2027. The chart workbook and quarterly table are distinct returned surfaces; exact reconciliation of every average is not asserted.

[EIA's November 4 inventory report](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/archive/2026/2026_11_04/download/summary.txt), covering October 30, reports a 3.6-million-barrel weekly distillate draw and stocks 13% below the five-year average. Refinery utilization was 94.5%. Four-week distillate product supplied rose 8.7% year over year. Product supplied is a balance measure, not proof of completed essential deliveries.

[IEA's October 14 assessment](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-october-2026) expects a global diesel deficit through Q4 and a surplus in Q1 2027 as refining recovers, but depleted stocks persist. September diesel exports were about 700,000 barrels/day below a year earlier. Higher freight costs could raise delivered prices in importing regions. Recovery depends partly on Middle Eastern product supply; stronger U.S./Chinese exports are a partial buffer. These are dated estimates and forecasts, not November measurements.

**Interpretation:** gradual U.S. easing is the baseline through January. A uniform worldwide decline is less defensible: import-dependent markets remain exposed to freight, supply disruption and winter demand. Cold weather, renewed outages or slower recovery could reverse the decline. Stock rebuilding, restored production and weaker demand would favor lower prices. No precise global pump-price forecast or current futures settlement was established.

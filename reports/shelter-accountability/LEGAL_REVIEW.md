# California shelter accountability — bounded legal scope review

Reviewer: camp_review. Reviewed October 20, 2026 UTC. Status: usable statutory screen with primary-text access limits; not a resident-specific legal conclusion. No private resident records examined. No finding of fraud, tenancy, unlawful termination or enforceability in a particular case.

## Scope first

Civil Code 1954.08(d), (e), (f), (g) defines the relevant program, administrator, operator and participant. Screen for listed public/CoC funding AND retained governmental/CoC oversight and accountability; identify the actual administrator and contracted operator (or same entity under 1954.09(b)). Occupancy must be solely due to participation in the defined program. The definition includes other shelter sites, not just hotels/motels. Do not label the chapter a universal rule for every organization calling itself a shelter. Conversely, the bill title's hotel/motel wording cannot narrow the operative definitions to motel programs alone.

1954.09(a) conditions the stated non-tenancy/non-hirer treatment on meeting ALL listed requirements, including Housing First and procedural safeguards. 1954.091(b)(1) preserves rights otherwise held under applicable law; (b)(2) sets out treatment and protections for participants in complying programs. Thus neither 'all shelter residents are tenants after 30 days' nor 'all shelter residents lack tenant rights' is supported. A missing safeguard warrants legal/contract review of the claimed exception, not an automated declaration that tenancy is created. Other applicable disability, funding, contract, local and housing protections remain separate questions.

1954.09(c) contains a permanent-housing conversion/certificate-of-occupancy provision using the bare cross-reference 'paragraph (2)'. Do not silently rewrite it as an unambiguous exemption of every safeguard or decide a converted site's tenancy from this screen. Preserve the text and flag conversion for specific review.

## Required process and narrow exceptions

| Issue | Bounded reading / case evidence to request |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary notice | 1954.09(a)(3)(A): written notice at least 30 days before proposed termination. Verify notice delivery and proposed termination dates; this is not a minimum length-of-stay rule. |
| Notice content | Plain-language reason, reasonable-accommodation right for qualified people with disabilities, and grievance right. Termination policy, notice procedure and grievance process disclosed at commencement; guideline violations include documented warnings/problem solving. |
| Direct threat | Immediate removal is permitted for a qualifying direct threat, with advice about grievance rights. A permissible termination ground is not automatically an immediate-removal exception. Cross-reference is 2 CCR 12179(b)(3), not an operator's unrestricted label. Significant harm/property risk, individualized evidence and possible mitigation/accommodation need scrutiny; do not decide an incident from an accusation. |
| Self-exit | 1954.08(c)/.09(a)(2)(D): voluntary departure plus no return or reasonable contact within 72 hours; operator must make good-faith location/voluntariness inquiries including hospitals, jails and relevant sites. An unexplained absence alone is not the complete statutory test. This exception concerns 30-day notice, not a blanket erasure of all duties. |
| Short extension | .09(a)(2)(E): where termination is due to program time limits and the existing stay is extended less than 30 days beyond original termination, an amended notice may add the extension's days. Not a general license to give every new termination less than 30 days. |
| Exit plan | Referrals to available eligible local services; documented good-faith reasonable effort to facilitate intake in an available bed/unit. Administrator evaluates those efforts when grievance used and makes good-faith reasonable transportation efforts. This is not a guaranteed replacement bed or housing outcome. |
| Exigent circumstances | If first-responder/police/fire circumstances make the letter/postexit coordination infeasible at that time, the operator must create compliant termination documentation and make it available within 24 hours of the participant's request. Do not misstate this as 24 hours after every removal or an unlimited waiver. |
| Appeal | Administrator establishes due-process grievance; operator discloses it. Opportunity begins 30 days before proposed termination or at any later point; direct-threat cases at removal or later. If termination not carried out following grievance, resume participation, or facilitate placement at an alternative available site if original placement unavailable. Text inspected does not supply a universal automatic stay pending appeal. |

## Dates and amendment lineage

- AB 1991, Stats 2022 chapter 645, established the framework; Judicial Council's official legislative summary identifies January 1, 2023 effectiveness. The 30-day rule did not originate in 2025 or 2026.
- AB 2835, Stats 2024 chapter 209, chaptered August 27, 2024 per Secretary of State. Amendments effective January 1, 2025 per code amendment notes; removed sunset and added self-exit/short-extension provisions, among changes. The June 2024 Senate Judiciary analysis already described existing definitions including other shelter sites and existing conditional shelter-site non-tenancy. Do not claim the 2024 amendment first broadened the entire framework beyond hotels.
- Current 1954.09 amendment note identifies Stats 2025 chapter 67 section 30, AB 1170, effective January 1, 2026. AB 1170 is code-maintenance legislation. This latest-version date must not be reported as the beginning of 30-day protections. Official final chapter text could not be opened in this run; no unsupported claim about every amendment's exact delta.

## One narrow case-screen

For ONE proposed termination at ONE publicly funded program, use nonidentifying program-level records first: current funding/operating agreement, administrator/operator identity, occupancy/program basis, policy packet, applicable version dates and any permanent-housing conversion. If coverage is supported, compare one redacted notice against reason, dates, claimed exception and accommodation/grievance/exit-plan documentation. Record each element as supported, contradicted or unknown. Route unresolved coverage/exception/tenancy interpretation for qualified review. A process gap is not itself proof of fraud, intent, universal practice or legal liability. No request or complaint is sent by this review.

## Final focused record review — October 20, 2026

Disposition: accepted as a bounded formal baseline and fictional triage input set, with the limits below. No material source-scope overclaim or fabricated practice finding identified in the reviewed files. This is a legal/data review, not acceptance of an implementation or a resident-specific legal opinion.

The catalogs distinguish reported version dates from the origin of the rule and retain official-current-text access uncertainty. The safeguard keeps actual operation, effectiveness and failure unknown. Both relay records document only expected transfers; actual origin, dispatch, arrival, action, outcome and verification remain unknown. Their empirical mode refers to the sourced legal baseline, not a real shelter event. The 11 rehearsal records explicitly identify fictional inputs and cannot count as cases, failures or a prevalence denominator.

The three coverage fields are an initial applicability screen only. They cannot establish satisfaction of all conditions for non-tenancy under section 1954.09(a), including Housing First in (a)(1). A future date/content evaluator must never infer lawful termination, full compliance or non-tenancy from those fields. An asserted exception is a review trigger, not proof of the exception. The schema deliberately lacks the evidence required to adjudicate direct threat, self-exit, extension eligibility, accommodation, service or conversion. Unknown legal version or scope must remain unresolved. A 30-day interval alone establishes no broader compliance.

Exigent paperwork concerns documentation timing, not an independently sufficient substantive ground for immediate removal. Preserve the request-triggered 24-hour deadline. The conversion cross-reference ambiguity, conditional rights, self-exit inquiries, extension prerequisites and absence of an automatic universal appeal stay must travel with downstream summaries. These are implementation guardrails, not defects in these explicitly limited input records.

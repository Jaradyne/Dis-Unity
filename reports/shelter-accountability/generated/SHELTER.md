## Scope and exceptions

The retained Civil Code §§1954.08–1954.091 text defines publicly funded/overseen programs, including other shelter sites as well as hotels/motels. Section 1954.09 conditions its stated non-tenancy treatment on all requirements, including Housing First; a funding/occupancy screen alone cannot establish that treatment. Existing rights remain preserved.

Within that framework, ordinary written notice is at least 30 days before proposed termination, with reasons, accommodation and grievance information. Qualifying direct threat, statutory self-exit and amendment of an already-noticed short extension require their own evidence. An exigent situation can affect when termination paperwork is practicable; it does not itself authorize every shortened notice. The specified fallback is availability within 24 hours of the participant’s request. Conversion provisions require separate interpretation.

The public administrator establishes the grievance process. Documented exit/intake efforts are required; a replacement bed is not guaranteed. The inspected text does not supply a universal automatic stay pending appeal. The 2026 version note is not the origin of 30-day protections; the official 2024 analysis already describes the rule.

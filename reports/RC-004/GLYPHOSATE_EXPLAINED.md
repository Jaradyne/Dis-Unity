# What “glyphosate health assessment” means

Public-source explanation checked 21 September 2026; not an individual medical assessment.

Glyphosate is an herbicide ingredient associated with Roundup products. A human-health assessment asks what harms the evidence supports, at which doses and exposures, and whether exposure through work, food, water or residential use requires protections. The ingredient, particular formulations, occupational exposure and ordinary dietary exposure are not interchangeable questions.

In 2015, IARC classified glyphosate as **probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A)**. That classification concerns carcinogenic hazard; it is not a statement that every exposure causes cancer or an estimate of any individual's probability. [IARC announcement](https://www.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/MonographVolume112-1.pdf).

EPA's earlier assessment reached a different cancer conclusion. Scope, exposure assessment, methodology and interpretation of evidence differ; describing the disagreement as only “hazard versus risk” would be incomplete. EPA's current page says it is updating its human-health assessment, with completion anticipated in late 2026. It also records the court's 2022 vacatur of the human-health portion of the 2020 interim decision and EPA's subsequent withdrawal of that interim decision. This is therefore an ongoing evidence/regulatory question, not a finished universal safety verdict. [EPA glyphosate page](https://www.epa.gov/ingredients-used-pesticide-products/glyphosate).

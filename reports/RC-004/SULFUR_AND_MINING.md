# Sulfur, mining and the Venezuelan construction clock — RC-004

Evidence cutoff: 21 September 2026. Bounded technical and historical review. This report identifies no new Venezuelan project and no available Venezuelan sulfur surplus.

**The useful answer is three different clocks: existing plant repair, a new recovery/treatment block, and a whole new oil/gas processing development.** They must not share one promised completion date.

## What would be built?

Sulfur in sour crude is embedded in compounds. Refining and gas treatment produce a hydrogen-sulfide stream; a Claus recovery unit converts that H2S into elemental sulfur. Tail-gas treatment, sulfur handling, utilities and a route to customers complete the service. Installing a Claus unit alone does not replace missing upstream separation or desulfurization capacity. [RC4S01]

Recovery produces a distinct, transportable commodity rather than requiring the fertilizer or mine operator to receive crude oil. However, this review has not assessed Venezuelan sulfur solidification, storage or port readiness. It does not label H2S, hot liquid sulfur, solid sulfur and sulfuric acid as interchangeable storage forms, nor give them a common safety or shelf-life claim.

**Planning inference:** if a new major recovery/treatment project were funded and able to proceed now, a roughly 3–5-year scenario is appropriate to test, not a promised Venezuelan delivery date. It is anchored to the 38-month announced upgrade and five-year integrated-build examples above. A project starting from an idea, requiring a new upgrader, hydrogen plant, reliable utilities or major export infrastructure can take longer. The fastest possibility is restoring usable existing capacity, but this review cannot honestly attach a number of months to that option.

Before narrowing the schedule, establish the site, current unit condition, sulfur-bearing feed rate, needed upstream treatment, engineering maturity, finance, equipment availability, construction workforce, environmental controls, power/water, storage and export loading. Lawful trade and payment access must also be checked against the transaction at the time; RC-003's dated licensing source is not project approval.

## What sulfur does for mining

Much of the relevant demand is for **sulfuric acid made from sulfur**, rather than elemental sulfur being poured into a mine.

- **Copper:** acid passes through suitable copper-bearing material and brings copper into solution. Solvent extraction separates it; electrowinning uses electricity to recover metal. Ore mineralogy determines the usable route. Sulfide concentrate smelting is a different pathway. [RC4S06]
- **Nickel and cobalt:** HPAL means high-pressure acid leaching. Acid, heat and pressure liberate metals from suitable low-grade oxide ore for further refining. It is one important processing family, not all nickel mining. [RC4S07]
- **Supply can also come from mining:** sulfur-bearing concentrate processing can produce sulfur dioxide that is recovered as sulfuric acid. SMM describes acid from Japanese copper processing going to its Philippine HPAL facilities, and sulfur from Japanese nickel processing being used there too. [RC4S05]

Acid circulation is not the same as acid consumption. EPA describes recycling leach liquids, but recycling does not establish zero fresh-acid demand. Reaction with other minerals, losses and impurity control affect the required make-up supply. This review has not quantified site-specific consumption or acid regeneration, so it will not infer tonnes of extra metal from a tonne of sulfur. [RC4S06; process inference]

This produces a real network opportunity: one industry's byproduct can support another's necessary function. It also creates a conditional cascade if an apparently separate acid supplier depends on the same smelter, refinery, gas field, electricity supply or shipping corridor.

## What can bridge waiting?

Only verified existing stocks, working plants with uncommitted output, usable routes and demand reductions can bridge an immediate shortfall. A new development should remain in the future-capacity column. Protect each supplier's existing commitments and local requirements before treating gross production as donor reserve.

Possible adaptations to investigate are recovery-unit maintenance, eligible smelter-acid supply, suitable spent-acid regeneration, material efficiency and recycling, and recovered nutrients where they genuinely displace required fertilizer. None is counted as currently spare here. Conserve avoidable transport fuel without assuming the needed sulfur coproduct will appear automatically elsewhere.

**Confirm the warning:** matched evidence of reduced sulfur/acid deliveries, declining usable stocks and plant curtailment after alternative routes and inputs are exhausted. **Falsify or downgrade it:** verified replacement deliveries and efficient demand reductions maintain essential fertilizer or metal output. Commodity price alone is insufficient.

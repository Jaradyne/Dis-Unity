# Fuel, physical flows and what can absorb waiting — RC-004

Evidence cutoff: 21 September 2026. This is a bounded public-source review, not a real-time fuel inventory service.

## What moved

The latest EIA weekly report opened here was released **16 September**, for the week ending **11 September**. U.S. distillate stocks were **107.9 million barrels**, up **1.6 million** that week but **13% below the seasonal five-year average**. Of these, 97.0 million barrels were in the 15-ppm-and-under category. Refinery utilization was **96.8%**; crude inputs were **17.3 million barrels/day**, and distillate output **5.2 million barrels/day**. Stock improvement and tightness coexist. ## Physical constraints and East Asia

The IEA's **11 September** public report estimates that observed global oil inventories fell **95 million barrels in August**. Gulf and Russian net diesel/gasoil exports together were **1.6 million barrels/day below February**. It reports refining losses in crude-importing Asia and higher freight costs. Its expectation of full Gulf supply recovery in 2027 is a forecast. The report also describes Brent futures around $105/barrel, $21 above early August, without the contract month and exact quote time needed for our reproducible futures series. [RC4F03](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-september-2026)

**Do not generalize that all East Asia is receiving less oil.** Japan's provisional August statistics put the PETROLEUM import quantity at **11,594 thousand kilolitres, up 3.6% year over year**, while value rose 58.7%. The petroleum-products row is separate. Imports are recorded by import-permit date. This is counterevidence to a blanket volume-decline claim, not proof that voyage times or future deliveries are normal. [RC4F05](https://www.customs.go.jp/toukei/shinbun/trade-st_e/2026/2026084e.pdf)

Specific country arrival delays, current Japanese reserve days and exportable East Asian product surplus remain unverified in this pass. A reported cargo purchase is not an arrived usable cargo.

## What can cover the delay?

Dividing U.S. stocks by four-week domestic distillate product supplied gives **107.9 / 3.602 ≈ 30 days**. This is our gross ratio, **not a 30-day exhaustion clock**. Weekly regional stocks were PADD1 21.6, PADD2 28.8, PADD3 43.8, PADD4 3.3 and PADD5 10.4 million barrels; totals do not establish local access. RC4F01.

The operational questions are: how much compatible fuel can actually be withdrawn, how quickly can it arrive, how much is already committed, and what must remain to keep the donor functioning? Pipelines, trucks, ports, fuel specification, credit and release authority all enter that calculation.

| Time layer | Candidate response | Evidence needed before counting it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Already available | Draw compatible stock above protected floors; reduce avoidable idling; combine loads | Accessible quantity, quality, essential burn rate, paid operators |
| Before next replenishment | Local swaps/trading; reroute deliveries; prioritized transport and compatible production | Confirmed origin, route, delivery window and donor floor |
| Weeks to months, project dependent | Repair capacity, suitable freight shifts, depot refrigeration shore power | Equipment, crew, connection and independently usable fallback |
| Longer structural replacement | Fleet/corridor electrification and new processing capacity | Commissioned function before old capacity is withdrawn |

These are planning layers, not universal construction promises. Swaps can save transport time but cannot manufacture new fuel. Extra crude requires working compatible refineries; it does not bypass a product shortage. High utilization is a reason to verify incremental output instead of treating nameplate capacity as reserve.

DOE describes about **one million barrels** of designated Northeast heating-oil reserve and emergency auction procedures. That is a specific regional buffer, not freely available fuel for California. Its usable contribution requires lawful release and delivery without exhausting the protected population. [RC4F04](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/opr/northeast-home-heating-oil-reserve)

A practical trigger is **replenishment arriving after usable stock would reach its protected floor**, especially with corroborating non-deliveries. A falsifier is timely independent replenishment followed by inventory rebuilding without cutting essential donor service. No local run-out date can currently be calculated.

## Next measurement

For one real transport or refrigeration service, obtain non-sensitive aggregate usable stock, essential daily demand, operating floor, confirmed resupply dates and alternatives. Model the next bridge before the current bridge is exhausted. Do not infer that a national stockpile, empty tank capacity, or a futures position is deliverable local fuel.

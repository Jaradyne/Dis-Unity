# What lasts, what replenishes, and what can arrive in time

RC-004; completed 22 September 2026, empirical evidence through 21 September. These are different clocks, not one resilience score.

| Clock | Question | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical shelf life | How long does the material remain suitable under actual storage conditions? | Assuming fast turnover means fast spoilage |
| Inventory turnover | How quickly is ordinary stock replaced or distributed? | Treating a month's throughput as a month held in reserve |
| Accessible service cover | How long can the actual stock support the required service? | Counting distant, committed, incompatible or inaccessible stock |
| Activation | How soon can a reserve start helping? | Counting a building when its doors are closed or operators absent |
| Replenishment | When will confirmed usable supplies arrive? | Counting an order, contract or cargo departure as arrival |
| Replacement | When can another system perform the function? | Expecting planting or construction to solve an immediate gap |

For positive net depletion, a first stock calculation is:

**Cover = (usable, compatible, accessible quantity − protected floor) ÷ (stressed use − dependable inflow).**

Quantities and flows need matching units and geography. If inflow meets demand, there is no finite depletion time under that assumption; this does not establish infinite security. Test what happens if the inflow fails. Food, water, power, labor, access or transport can end useful service before material is exhausted.

| Resource or response | What is known | What still limits the bridge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food already in a node | Actual local usable quantity and demand unknown | Diet, access, water, cooking, refrigeration, distribution and replacement deliveries |
| Commercial shelf-stable food | USDA gives 2–5 years of quality storage for intact properly stored low-acid cans; high-acid products often less | Years of product life is not years of inventory. Opening, damage or poor storage changes suitability |
| Diesel stocks | US gross stock/use ratio about 30 days using Sept 11 stock and recent domestic supply measure | Local stocks after floors, continuing production/imports/exports, specification, pipeline/terminal access and demand |
| Battery reserve | No specific site's useful energy/essential load verified | Usable energy divided by actual critical load; recharge, weather, islanding, access and maintenance |
| Quick vegetables | UC gives radish harvest at 3–6 weeks under suitable conditions | Preparation, water, weather, nutrients, yield and incomplete nutrition |
| Tomatoes | Warm-season crop with region-specific planting dates | Planting is not harvest; a December route needs actual warm-region/protected production and weather-safe transport |
| Major new sulfur capacity | Historical projects indicate years; 3–5 years is only a rough major-project scenario | Site condition, funding, scope, feed, hydrogen/utilities, commissioning and export capacity |
| Rail electrification | Caltrain construction-to-full-service benchmark about seven years | Earlier planning excluded; project-specific scope and continuing service obligations |

[USDA storage guidance](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/shelf-stable-food); [UC radishes](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/cultural-tips-for-growing-radish/); [UC tomatoes](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/cultural-tips-for-growing-tomato/); [fuel detail](FUEL_AND_WAITING.md); [sulfur detail](SULFUR_AND_MINING.md); [rail detail](RAIL_TRANSITION.md).

## Food banks and production

Food assistance is not uniformly financed by rescued surplus: purchasing and government commodities also matter. Nevertheless, a broad physical food disruption could weaken multiple inflows while demand rises. An income/distribution crisis can instead coexist with food elsewhere. Neither scenario is established simply by high food-bank demand.

The proposed bridge is overlapping: food available now; protected rotating staples and diversified producers next; successive seasonal crops and storage crops as they mature; reciprocal regional exchange. Gardens can expand agency and useful nutrition. They still require seed, water, nutrients, tools, labor and time. Producing without relying on every market transaction does not mean producing without inputs.

Fast radishes and leafy vegetables help variety and micronutrient supply; they are not a full caloric/protein reserve. Tomatoes are valuable but are not the default fastest emergency crop. A proposed California-to-cold-region winter route needs dated harvest volume, donor needs, temperature-appropriate storage/transport, weather access and an able receiver. No operating winter tomato corridor has been verified here.

For soil, combine soil testing, crop need and compost analysis. Organic nitrogen release depends on temperature, moisture and decomposition; enough compost to satisfy nitrogen alone can add excessive phosphorus or potassium. Nutrient recycling reduces losses but does not create those elements. [UC nutrient guidance](https://ucanr.edu/site/solution-center-nutrient-management/nitrogen-organic-sources).

Oakland has a concrete compost return point at **West Oakland Farm Park, 2847 Peralta Street**, through Acta Non Verba: posted Tuesday–Friday 1–6 pm and Saturday 10 am–2 pm, bring containers/self-load, available stock can run out. The City says it is made from food scraps and yard waste collected from Oakland homes and businesses. The complete current route of every municipal green-bin load remains open. Altamont's described processing design capacity is not a current spare-compost allocation. [City service page](https://www.oaklandca.gov/My-Household/Waste-and-Recycling/Free-Compost).

## Similar expectation gaps

| Expected fallback | How the same stress can impair it | More useful design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food donations | Donors lose supply or purchasing power as need rises | Protected procurement, rotating food, production and diverse routes |
| Fuel generator | Fuel deliveries fail with the grid or transport disruption | Measured onsite usable fuel, load shedding and differently supplied fallback |
| Electricity imports | Neighbors share the heat wave or transmission constraint | Donor-floor accounting, deliverable capacity and local critical loads |
| Library or hub | Closure, reduced hours, exclusion or inaccessible travel | Indoor/outdoor/mobile partners with different access failure modes |
| Volunteers | They face the same illness, displacement or income pressure | Protect people's capacity; pay necessary roles where funded and share knowledge |
| Insurance or emergency finance | Cash is delayed or cannot buy scarce physical equipment | Maintain physical repair options and distinguish liquidity from service capacity |
| Nutrient coproduct | Parent industrial production shrinks | Map alternate recovery, material efficiency and compatible suppliers before withdrawal |

These are conditional mechanisms, not claims that each is currently failing.

Bay Area food-service research is a convenient local demonstration, not an assessment that it is the most vulnerable place. Rural Tennessee comparison should examine county-level travel, delivery frequency, inventory and alternatives. Mobile equipment, service crews, seedling nurseries or seasonal exchange can link settled nodes without presuming mass migration.

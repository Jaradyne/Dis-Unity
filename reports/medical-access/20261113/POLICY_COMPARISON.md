# One provider, one payer: distinct services and evidence limits

Work Aiden, November 15, 2026 Pacific. A bounded public-policy comparison of UCSF and Alameda Alliance Medi-Cal. [Source ledger](policy-sources.json) preserves versions, locators, inspection dates and source families. These descriptions are not patient records or evidence of delivered care.

| Policy surface | Published distinction | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [UCSF specialty appointments](https://ohns.ucsf.edu/about/contact-us/make-appointment) | The department may request a formal referral and clinical reason, relevant records and insurance authorization where applicable. | This describes intake, not confirmed receipt, acceptance or a usable appointment. |
| [UCSF Tinnitus Care](https://www.ucsfhealth.org/care/services/tinnitus-care) | Medical evaluation by an otologist is described separately from audiology management. The latter lists initial counseling around $300 and follow-up $100–$200; some tests may be covered depending on plan and authorization. | Published estimates are not actual bills or a blanket exclusion of diagnostic care. |
| [UCSF EARS group](https://ears.ucsf.edu/en/tinnitus) | The education group is self-pay, listed at $65, without a referral or prior audiogram prerequisite. Individual medical evaluation has a separate scheduling/authorization route. | Group access does not establish specialist assessment or replace the requested medical service. |
| [Alliance 2026 handbook](https://alamedaalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/MCAL-MBR_2026-Alliance-EOC_10242025-clean-current-.pdf), printed pp.39, 52, 80 | Describes specialist referrals and possible approved out-of-network care when needed services are unavailable in-network. Audiology is covered, with a combined two-service monthly limit for adults and possible authorization for more; the limit does not apply under age21. | Coverage categories do not establish that a particular UCSF clinician, code or appointment is authorized. |
| [Alliance erratum2](https://alamedaalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/D.0058C_20260715_AAH_Mcal_EOC_Errata2Cln.pdf), pp.1–2 | Specifies prior authorization for certain services, including specialized tests; retains emergency/sensitive-care exceptions and notice/appeal language. | A published review procedure is not a completed authorization, accessible appeal or remedy. |

The [payer index](https://alamedaalliance.org/members/medi-cal/benefits-and-covered-services/) lists three corrections alongside the 2026 handbook. Errata1 and2 revise authorization text; erratum3 addresses another benefit category. Filename dates are version clues, not independently verified publication/effective dates. This packet does not encode a deadline from an older handbook or assume that a plan-wide category resolves service-code requirements.

**Assessment:** a self-pay education or counseling offer does not establish that medical diagnostic care is unavailable or uncovered. Equally, descriptions of evaluation and coverage do not demonstrate accessible care. Service matching, network, authorization, cost, communication usability, attendance and clinical outcomes remain unverified for any individual. The documents establish neither wrongdoing nor a successful remedy. Clinical uncertainty is distinct from an institutional access failure.

**One next public-source test:** match one diagnostic service on an official provider referral form to one current payer authorization entry by service code, effective date and network conditions. An unresolved match stays UNKNOWN. No individual records, outreach or automated eligibility decision are part of that proposal.

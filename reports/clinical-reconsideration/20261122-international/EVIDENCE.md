# What reached the chart, the clinician and the patient?

Companion to [Getting a second opinion when the diagnosis is still open](README.md). First published November 22, 2026. Updated November 23, Pacific.

## When the team that gave the first opinion controlled the referral

In a California enforcement case, a medical group wanted to defer surgery for a child with a rare disease. When the family asked for an outside review, the group said the current care was appropriate. It offered no other specialist and gave no written denial. The family contacted the plan or the group about 16 times in the six months after the first denial. More than six months after the original request, Kaiser Foundation Health Plan authorized an outside referral. Its specialists then met with outside specialists and drew up a plan for prompt surgery. The public letter documents the revised treatment plan. The child's eventual surgical and health outcomes remain unknown from these records. [DMHC enforcement letter, matter 15-113, pp. 1–3](https://wpso.dmhc.ca.gov/enfactions/docs/2709/1603903017535.pdf)

The California Department of Managed Health Care (DMHC) imposed a **$225,000 penalty** in 2016. Its annual report also documents a change in who could receive a request. Kaiser members gained a route through the plan's Member Services department that bypassed the original medical group. The case shows what a public enforcement record can establish about one family and a plan's procedures. [Enforcement docket, September 14, 2016](https://wpso.dmhc.ca.gov/enfactions/actiondisplay.aspx?ActionKey=2709&LinkKey=6332) · [DMHC 2016 annual report, enforcement highlights](https://www.dmhc.ca.gov/Portals/0/FileAComplaint/DMHCDecisionsAndReports/AnnualComplaintAndIMRDecisions/2016.pdf)

## A real dispute over what a patient could see

In January 2026 Ontario's Information and Privacy Commissioner decided a case about an Ottawa Hospital after-visit summary. The hospital kept the original summary and attached the patient's disagreement in Epic, its clinical record system. The patient could see the summary in MyChart but could not see the disagreement there. The adjudicator found the hospital met its duty by attaching the statement to the summary and ensuring it would accompany release of that information through its records department. The decision documents the patient's portal experience and the hospital's disclosure procedure. It leaves open whether another treating clinician read the statement, reconsidered the assessment or changed care. [Ontario PHIPA Decision 326, especially paragraphs 35–39](https://www.ipc.on.ca/en/decisions/latest-decisions/phipa-decision-326)

A U.S. veterans' emergency-department study examined patients with heart failure who came in with shortness of breath. When the triage note mentioned heart failure, clinicians tested for a blood clot in the lung less often. Among patients who had a test, more time elapsed from arrival to testing. By 30 days, the difference in recorded clot diagnoses between the groups was statistically inconclusive. An early label was associated with how clinicians investigated the symptom. The study could not determine which individual patients had a clot that went undetected. [Primary study, results and limitations](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2806464) · [AHRQ Patient Safety Network summary](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/evidence-anchoring-bias-during-physician-decision-making)

## How to challenge an entry

A wrong test date, an accurately recorded opinion the patient disputes, and a later clinician's different assessment raise three distinct questions. Here is what a patient can request from the organization holding the record.

| System | What can go into the record or accompany disclosure? |
| --- | --- |
| **U.S. / California** | After inspecting their records, a California patient can submit a signed, dated addendum of up to **250 words per disputed item**, clearly asking for inclusion. The provider must attach it and include it when disclosing that portion to a third party. Under federal HIPAA, a patient can also request an amendment and submit a statement of disagreement after denial, subject to the federal rule. [Medical Board of California](https://www.mbc.ca.gov/FAQs/default.aspx?cat=Consumer&topic=Complaint%3A+Medical+Records) · [45 CFR §164.526](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.526) |
| **Canada / Ontario** | After obtaining access, ask the record holder in writing to correct inaccurate or incomplete information. Good-faith clinical opinions are an exception to mandatory correction. After a refusal the patient can require a statement of disagreement to accompany the disputed information. If the requested correction could reasonably affect ongoing care or benefits, the patient can also require the record holder to make all reasonable efforts to send the statement to previous recipients of the disputed information. [Ontario privacy commissioner](https://www.ipc.on.ca/en/health-organizations/access-and-correction/correction) · [Decision about a patient portal](https://www.ipc.on.ca/en/decisions/latest-decisions/phipa-decision-326) |
| **England** | A patient can ask the NHS organization to correct an inaccuracy or include their account. An opinion justified on the information available at the time can stay in the chronology, with a subsequent diagnosis or the patient's disagreement alongside it. [NHS England guidance](https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/information-governance/guidance/amending-patient-and-service-user-records) |
| **Germany** | A patient can request rectification of a factual error, including an incorrect fact on which a diagnosis rests. Revisiting a medical judgment generally requires a clinical assessment. Section 630f requires an amendment to preserve the original content and the timing of the change. [Lower Saxony data protection authority, question 34](https://www.lfd.niedersachsen.de/download/184759/FAQ_DS-GVO_im_Gesundheitsbereich_V_3.0_PDF_-_nicht_vollstaendig_barrierefrei_.pdf) · [Civil Code §630f](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__630f.html) |
| **Sweden** | If the patient considers an entry incorrect or misleading, that view must be recorded. A correction identifies when it was made and by whom. [Patient Data Act, chapter 3, §§8 and 14](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/patientdatalag-2008355_sfs-2008-355/) |
| **Japan** | The Personal Information Protection Commission explains how to request correction of inaccurate chart facts. For private operators covered by the cited rule, its guidance distinguishes an assessment from an erroneous fact on which that assessment rests. A disagreement with a clinician's judgment can therefore require a separate clinical review. [Medical records FAQ](https://www.ppc.go.jp/all_fad_index/fad1-2-2-q6/) · [Commission's distinction between facts and assessments](https://www.ppc.go.jp/all_faq_index/faq1-q9-19/) |
| **Australia** | Where federal privacy law applies, including most private providers, patients can ask for a correction. If refused, they may ask for a statement of disagreement to be linked to the record. After a correction, they can request notification of prior recipients. Public hospitals follow state or territory rules. In New South Wales, a patient whose requested amendment is declined can ask to have their statement attached so it can be read with the disputed entry. [Federal privacy commissioner](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/health-information/correct-your-health-information) · [NSW Health privacy manual, §12.8](https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/policies/manuals/Documents/privacy-manual-for-health-information.pdf) |
| **Brazil** | Article 19 of the Patient Rights Statute lets a patient see their chart without giving a reason, obtain a free copy and request a correction. The General Data Protection Law also covers inaccurate, incomplete or outdated data. Following a correction, the controller must promptly inform the other organizations that received the data for processing, subject to exceptions for proven impossibility or disproportionate effort. [Patient Rights Statute, art. 19](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei/2026/lei-15378-6-abril-2026-798918-publicacaooriginal-178753-pl.html) · [General Data Protection Law, art. 18(III) and §6](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm) |

## What patients did with a second opinion

A U.S. employee benefit allowed people to request a specialist's review of their case. Across **6,791 completed reviews** in 2011–2012, the second opinion recommended a different diagnosis in 14.8% and a change in treatment in 37.4%. These groups overlap. Of 6,248 people sent a survey, 2,683 responded. Among those answering the relevant item, **2,242 of 2,681** said they discussed the review with their physician. On a separate item, **1,641 of 2,680** said they planned to follow the recommendations. [Meyer and colleagues, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25913850/) · [Supplementary Table 1, patient survey](https://www.royaldoctors.com/content/documents/studiemodule/evaluation-of-outcomes-from-a-natilonal-patient-initiated-so-program.pdf)

The responses reveal how often patients said the conversation happened. The study did not independently verify which opinion was correct, whether the treating physician changed care, or whether symptoms improved. People who could not obtain a review are outside the completed-case count.

## Evidence that people got through

| Public evidence | What was observed | What question stays open? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **U.S. Undiagnosed Diseases Network**, study published 2018 | Of **1,519 referred**, 601 were accepted. Of 382 whose evaluation was complete by the study cutoff, 132 were diagnosed. A therapy change was recommended for 28. Researchers observed a positive treatment effect in 8 of those 28. Six had an unclear or negative effect, four had yet to start treatment, and the outcome was undetermined for ten. [Open study, effects on medical care](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9cq331ng) · [Current application requires a provider letter](https://undiagnosed.hms.harvard.edu/participate/) | What happened to people who never obtained the letter, and to those outside the completed evaluations? This selected research cohort measures more stages than most service descriptions. |
| **Germany, Bonn rare-disease centre**, 2023 | Of 422 inquiries, 98 advanced to further case work and 82 received a direct recommendation. The centre also recorded 60 outpatient visits and 55 video consultations. Genetic testing clarified 63 previously unresolved cases that year. [Centre's 2023 quality report, pp. 7–8 and 45](https://zseb.ukbonn.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Qualitaetsbericht_2023_ZSEB.pdf) | The report gives separate counts. It does not establish how many of the 422 inquirers received a diagnosis or experienced relief. |
| **England, 100,000 Genomes pilot**, 2021 | Among 2,183 selected families, 533 received a genetic diagnosis. Clinicians judged 134 of the diagnoses immediately actionable. For one child, identifying CTPS1 deficiency enabled a curative bone-marrow transplant. [Pilot study and clinical examples](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7613219/) | Which other patients benefited, and how? “Actionable” included several kinds of consequences, including family or reproductive decisions. |
| **Japan, IRUD**, six years through March 2021 | Of 6,301 enrolled families, analysis was complete for 5,136. Investigators identified a cause in 2,247 of the analyzed families (43.8%). [National Center of Neurology and Psychiatry results](https://www.ncnp.go.jp/topics/2022/20220405p.html) | What happened to families outside the completed analyses, and did a diagnosis improve care? The unit counted was a family. |
| **Brazil, RARAS registry**, study published 2026 | Researchers identified 18,625 participants, 12,048 with confirmed diagnoses, and 5,984 eligible for the interval analysis. In that last group the median time from symptom onset to diagnosis was **2.94 years**, and the mean was **6.21 years**. [Primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42742802/) | How long have people still without a diagnosis been waiting? They cannot contribute a completed interval to this analysis. |

These figures measure different events in different populations. None estimates the chance that any given person will obtain an independent second opinion. For the specific problem of a disputed first assessment, two especially informative missing counts are how many people asked for another review but never reached one, and how often the next clinician examined the underlying material and explained their reasoning.

California also makes certain individual **coverage decisions** publicly searchable. The Department of Managed Health Care publishes independent medical review decisions involving plan denials, delays or modifications. A decision in the enrollee's favor obliges the plan to authorize the requested service. To find out whether the patient attended or benefited requires further evidence. [DMHC search](https://www.dmhc.ca.gov/DataResearch/IndependentMedicalReviewandComplaintReports.aspx) · [State data and definitions](https://data.ca.gov/%40california-department-of-managed-health-care/independent-medical-review-imr-determinations-trend)

**How to read this comparison.** Laws and regulator guidance describe duties. Provider and plan materials document what those institutions publish. A registry, a cohort and a decided complaint each show what happened to a defined group or person. Source links identify the relevant jurisdiction, service or study. The inspection used public pages and documents. A directly observed outcome for routine outpatient second opinions across all eight systems was unavailable in the sources reviewed.

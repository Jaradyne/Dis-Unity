# Getting a second opinion when the diagnosis is still open

Public guide and comparison of eight health systems. Sources checked November 22, 2026, Pacific.

A patient can be too ill to carry on normally and still have no agreed diagnosis. If the first clinician also controls the next referral, where can that person turn? California allows members of covered health plans to request another opinion when the clinical picture is unclear. In Sweden, the formal duty to help arrange a new assessment depends on the seriousness of the condition. In Bonn, Germany, an adult can describe the problem in their own words, but needs a GP's participation for the rare-disease centre to consider the file. These differences matter before anyone gets into an examination room. [California law, §1383.15](https://california.public.law/codes/health_and_safety_code_section_1383.15) · [Sweden's 1177 patient guide](https://www.1177.se/sa-fungerar-varden/om-du-inte-ar-nojd/ny-medicinsk-bedomning/) · [Bonn adult intake](https://zseb.ukbonn.de/patienten/ohne-diagnose-erwachsene/)

## Who can get another look?

| System | Who can start it and who controls access? | What can the person actually get? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **United States — California** | Under §1383.15 an enrollee in a covered health care service plan can request a qualified second opinion. The grounds expressly include unclear or complex findings, conflicting tests and a treating professional unable to identify the condition. | Alameda Alliance's Medi-Cal materials let members request an in-network opinion directly and waive plan preapproval for it. If the network cannot provide the opinion, the plan describes an approved outside route. UCSF offers an appointment and a separately priced written review. [Statute](https://california.public.law/codes/health_and_safety_code_section_1383.15) · [Alliance member notice, pp. 2–3](https://alamedaalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/D.0058C_20260715_AAH_Mcal_EOC_Errata2Cln.pdf) · [UCSF options](https://www.ucsfhealth.org/patients-visitors/second-opinion) |
| **Canada — Ontario** | A family doctor or specialist can refer for a provincially covered second opinion. The receiving consultant must tell the referring clinician whether they accept within 14 days, sooner if urgent. | That gives the patient and referrer a decision to follow up. The appointment has its own timetable. Care4Rare, a separate research program for suspected single-gene conditions, accepts referrals from collaborating clinicians at participating sites. [Canadian Medical Association](https://www.cma.ca/healthcare-for-real/how-do-second-opinions-work) · [Ontario regulator](https://www.cpso.on.ca/physicians-physician-assistants/professional-resources/professional-obligations/clinical-care/Obligations-ensuring-smooth-transitions-in-care) · [Care4Rare](https://www.care4rare.ca/contact-impact) |
| **England** | A patient can ask a consultant or GP for another NHS opinion. The Royal Free explains that doctors should respect the request. A patient seeking a routine outpatient consultation has no general legal entitlement to a second NHS opinion. | Clinical genetics is one narrower route. Great Ormond Street sees adults and children and invites clinician contact about a significant personal or family rare-disease history after negative testing. Eligibility and referral still matter. [Royal Free patient guide](https://www.royalfree.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-leaflets/changing-your-nhs-hospital-doctor-or-asking-second-opinion) · [Clinical genetics](https://www.gosh.nhs.uk/wards-and-departments/departments/clinical-specialties/clinical-genetics/conditions-we-treat/) |
| **Germany** | The formal statutory second-opinion program covers specified planned procedures. Bonn has a different route for people whose symptoms span specialties and whose clinician suspects a rare disease. Its adult service asks for the person's account, a recent report, a GP form and a GP referral. | After triage, Bonn can recommend another route or take the case forward. For the latter it describes an in-person or video consultation, an interdisciplinary conference, and a report to both patient and GP. It warns processing can take up to six months. [Federal Joint Committee](https://www.g-ba.de/richtlinien/107/) · [Who Bonn sees](https://zseb.ukbonn.de/patienten/) · [Bonn adult intake](https://zseb.ukbonn.de/patienten/ohne-diagnose-erwachsene/) |
| **Sweden** | For a life-threatening or particularly serious illness or injury, the responsible clinician must help find another clinic and refer. The home region pays for the qualifying assessment and travel to another region if needed. | Some clinical services also accept patient-written referrals for triage. Karolinska's Centre for Rare Diseases welcomes direct contact to locate expertise and explicitly provides guidance only. A clinical department must do the assessment. [1177](https://www.1177.se/sa-fungerar-varden/om-du-inte-ar-nojd/ny-medicinsk-bedomning/) · [Karolinska self-referrals](https://www.karolinska.se/vard/patient-och-besokare/sok-vard-hos-oss/) · [Centre's remit](https://www.karolinska.se/vard/funktion/funktion-medicinsk-diagnostik-karolinska/vardgivare/universitetslaboratoriet/vara-verksamheter-kul/centrum-for-sallsynta-diagnoser/kontakt/om-oss/) |
| **Japan** | Nagoya University's second-opinion clinic uses the patient's interview and records from their treating doctor. For a different service, Osaka's IRUD research site asks the treating clinician to refer an eligible undiagnosed patient. | Nagoya gives advice without new tests or treatment at that consultation. The IRUD program can investigate qualifying cases. Its published criteria generally require unexplained symptoms that interfere with daily life for at least six months (the time threshold is waived for infants), plus either unexplained objective findings across organ systems or suspicion of a genetic cause. [Nagoya clinic](https://www.med.nagoya-u.ac.jp/hospital_en/guide/outpatient/secondopinion/) · [Osaka IRUD](https://irud.amed365.jp/patient/) · [IRUD patient guide in English](https://irud.umin.jp/en/) |
| **Australia** | A patient can ask their doctor to refer them to another specialist. For an ordinary specialist consultation Medicare funding generally requires a valid referral. | As one public example, Nepean Blue Mountains in New South Wales accepts adults and children with a known or suspected genetic condition, or at risk of one. A GP or specialist must refer before an appointment can be made. [Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/healthcare-and-services/general-health/what-is-a-referral) · [NSW public genetics clinic](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/nbmlhd/service-directory/genetics) |
| **Brazil** | Under article 39 of the national medical ethics code, a physician may not oppose a second opinion requested by the patient or their legal representative. For rare disease in the SUS public system, primary care is the usual point of entry and coordinates onward care. | Specialist and reference services can investigate suspected rare diseases. Local referral arrangements and capacity determine who gets seen. The ethical duty alone supplies no appointment date. [Medical ethics code, art. 39](https://bvsms.saude.gov.br/bvs/publicacoes/codigo_etica_medica.pdf) · [Ministry of Health care pathway](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/composicao/saes/doencas-raras/linha-de-cuidados) |

These are examples at the level named in each row. A genetics research program has eligibility criteria of its own. Someone whose symptoms are confined to one organ, or whose condition may be acquired, can still have a valid question for a second clinician. The general second-opinion route and the rare-disease program answer different requests.

## A concrete route in the East Bay

For an **Alameda Alliance Medi-Cal member**, the 2026 notice gives Member Services numbers **510-747-4567** and **877-932-2738**. A member can ask the plan to identify a qualified clinician who can review the unresolved question. If the plan denies the request, the notice describes a grievance. UCSF lists an appointment that can be billed to insurance or paid out of pocket, and a separate flat-fee written opinion. The plan can confirm network and coverage for a specific service. [Alliance notice, “Second opinions”](https://alamedaalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/D.0058C_20260715_AAH_Mcal_EOC_Errata2Cln.pdf) · [UCSF comparison](https://www.ucsfhealth.org/patients-visitors/second-opinion)

For plans covered by **California Health and Safety Code §1383.15**, a second opinion about specialist care can come from a different medical group in the network, in the same or an equivalent specialty. If the network has no appropriately qualified reviewer, the plan must authorize one outside it. The plan must require the reviewer to give **both the patient and the initial clinician** the consultation report, including any tests or procedures the reviewer recommends. A plan that denies a second opinion must give the patient written reasons and explain the grievance right. Those provisions give the patient identifiable documents to request. [California law, subsections (f)–(i)](https://california.public.law/codes/health_and_safety_code_section_1383.15) · [California regulator’s codebook, §1383.15](https://www.dmhc.ca.gov/Portals/0/Docs/OLS/2025%20Knox-Keene%20Act%20and%20Title%2028%20Book/CA%20Knoxx-Keene%20Act%202025%20Edition_508r.pdf)

One way to make a review inspectable is to ask which original test, image, result or observation the next clinician examined, what still does not fit, and who will follow up. If the concern is an inaccurate entry, ask who can see the addendum when that entry is shared. Each question corresponds to a different event the Garden can document.

# Getting a second opinion when the diagnosis is still open

Public guide to eight health systems. First published November 22, 2026. Updated November 23, Pacific.

A patient can be too ill to carry on normally and still have no agreed diagnosis. If the clinician who gave the first opinion also controls the next referral, who else can open the door? In California, a member can go directly to a covered health plan. In Sweden, patients can submit their own referrals to some clinics. In Germany, patients can generally consult another outpatient doctor, although a rare-disease centre in Bonn requires a GP's participation. ## Who can get another look?

| System | Who can start it and who controls access? | What can the person actually get? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **United States — California** | Under §1383.15 an enrollee in a covered health care service plan can request a qualified second opinion. The grounds expressly include unclear or complex findings, conflicting tests and a treating professional unable to identify the condition. | Alameda Alliance's Medi-Cal materials let members request an in-network opinion directly and waive plan preapproval for it. If the network cannot provide the opinion, the plan describes an approved outside route. UCSF offers an appointment and a separately priced written review. [Statute](https://california.public.law/codes/health_and_safety_code_section_1383.15) · [Alliance member notice, pp. 2–3](https://alamedaalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/D.0058C_20260715_AAH_Mcal_EOC_Errata2Cln.pdf) · [UCSF options](https://www.ucsfhealth.org/patients-visitors/second-opinion) |
| **Canada — Ontario** | A family doctor or specialist can refer for a provincially covered second opinion. The receiving consultant must tell the referrer within **14 days** whether they will accept, sooner if urgent. | If accepted, the consultant must tell the patient the expected wait or appointment details unless the referrer has agreed to do so. Care4Rare, a research program for suspected single-gene conditions, takes referrals from collaborating clinicians at participating sites. [Canadian Medical Association](https://www.cma.ca/healthcare-for-real/how-do-second-opinions-work) · [Ontario regulator, obligations 39–40](https://www.cpso.on.ca/physicians-physician-assistants/professional-resources/professional-obligations/clinical-care/Obligations-ensuring-smooth-transitions-in-care) · [Care4Rare](https://www.care4rare.ca/contact-impact) |
| **England** | A patient can ask a consultant or GP. Doctors must respect a patient's right to seek another opinion. For routine NHS outpatient care there is no general entitlement to a second consultation. The Royal Free advises that another consultant will usually require a fresh GP referral. | Great Ormond Street's clinical genetics service sees adults and children. After negative testing, clinicians can still discuss referral where a significant personal or family history suggests a rare condition. [General Medical Council, paragraph 18](https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/the-professional-standards/good-medical-practice/domain-2-patients-partnership-and-communication) · [Royal Free patient guide](https://www.royalfree.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-leaflets/changing-your-nhs-hospital-doctor-or-asking-second-opinion) · [Clinical genetics](https://www.gosh.nhs.uk/wards-and-departments/departments/clinical-specialties/clinical-genetics/conditions-we-treat/) |
| **Germany** | A person with statutory insurance can generally ask another participating outpatient doctor for advice. People who opt into GP-centred care usually need a GP referral for a specialist. A separate statutory program covers specified planned procedures. [Federal health portal](https://gesund.bund.de/wege-im-gesundheitswesen/erwachsenenleben/patientenrechte/arztwahl-und-selbstbestimmung) · [GP-centred care](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/themen/krankenversicherung/grundprinzipien/freie-arztwahl) · [Formal second-opinion program](https://www.g-ba.de/richtlinien/107/) | Bonn's adult rare-disease centre asks for the patient's account, a recent medical report, a GP form and a GP referral. Processing can take up to six months. Cases taken forward can lead to a consultation, a multidisciplinary conference and a written report for both the patient and GP. [Who Bonn sees](https://zseb.ukbonn.de/patienten/) · [Adult intake](https://zseb.ukbonn.de/patienten/ohne-diagnose-erwachsene/) |
| **Sweden** | For a life-threatening or particularly serious illness or injury, the clinician responsible must help find another clinic and arrange a referral. The home region pays for the qualifying assessment and any necessary travel to another region. [1177 patient guide](https://www.1177.se/sa-fungerar-varden/om-du-inte-ar-nojd/ny-medicinsk-bedomning/) | Patients can also submit their own requests to some clinics. Karolinska's clinical genetics clinic in Solna offers a general genetics self-referral through 1177. The clinic assesses the request. The separate Centre for Rare Diseases helps patients locate expertise. [Solna self-referral form listing](https://www.1177.se/hitta-vard/kontaktkort/Mottagning-Klinisk-genetik-Solna-Karolinska-Universitetssjukhuset/) · [Karolinska's referral process](https://www.karolinska.se/vard/patient-och-besokare/sok-vard-hos-oss/) · [Centre's remit](https://www.karolinska.se/vard/funktion/funktion-medicinsk-diagnostik-karolinska/vardgivare/universitetslaboratoriet/vara-verksamheter-kul/centrum-for-sallsynta-diagnoser/kontakt/om-oss/) |
| **Japan** | At Nagoya University Hospital, the patient needs a clinical letter and test records from the treating doctor. The relevant department decides whether to accept the request. The visit provides advice on the existing evidence, without new tests or treatment. [Nagoya intake requirements](https://www.med.nagoya-u.ac.jp/hospital/guide/outpatient/secondopinion/) · [English clinic overview](https://www.med.nagoya-u.ac.jp/hospital_en/guide/outpatient/secondopinion/) | For suspected undiagnosed disease, the treating doctor can apply to the IRUD research program. Its board decides admission. Eligibility generally requires persistent unexplained symptoms affecting daily life (usually at least six months, with an exception for infants), plus unexplained objective findings across organ systems or a suspected genetic cause. [Osaka IRUD](https://irud.amed365.jp/patient/) · [National patient guide](https://plaza.umin.ac.jp/irud/en/index.php) |
| **Australia** | A patient can ask their doctor for another specialist referral. An ordinary Medicare-funded specialist visit generally requires a valid referral. The referral can leave the specialist unnamed, so the patient can choose where to present it. [Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/healthcare-and-services/general-health/what-is-a-referral) · [Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/referrals-for-specialist-treatment?context=20) | The Nepean Blue Mountains public genetics service in New South Wales sees adults and children with a known or suspected genetic condition, or at risk of one. A GP or specialist must refer before an appointment can be made. [Clinic's intake criteria](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/nbmlhd/service-directory/genetics) |
| **Brazil** | Since April 2026, federal Law 15.378 has expressly given patients the right to seek another professional's or service's opinion about their health or a recommended procedure at any stage of treatment. Article 18 includes an emergency exception. The statute covers public and private care. [Patient Rights Statute, arts. 3 and 18](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/lei/2026/lei-15378-6-abril-2026-798918-publicacaooriginal-178753-pl.html) | In the SUS public system, primary care is the usual entry point for suspected rare disease and coordinates referrals. Specialist and reference services investigate eligible cases. The pathway still depends on local referral arrangements. [Ministry of Health care pathway](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/composicao/saes/doencas-raras/linha-de-cuidados) |

These are examples at the level named in each row. A genetics research program has eligibility criteria of its own. Someone whose symptoms are confined to one organ, or whose condition may be acquired, can still have a valid question for a second clinician. The general second-opinion route and the rare-disease program answer different requests.

## A concrete route in the East Bay

An **Alameda Alliance Medi-Cal member** can call Member Services directly at **510-747-4567** or **877-932-2738** and ask the plan to find a qualified clinician for a second opinion. The member can start the request even if the first specialist declines to refer. The Alliance covers an in-network second opinion without plan preapproval and describes a grievance route if it denies the request. [Alliance member notice, “Second opinions,” pp. 2–3](https://alamedaalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/D.0058C_20260715_AAH_Mcal_EOC_Errata2Cln.pdf)

UCSF accepts requests for a clinical appointment with or without an existing diagnosis. The visit can be billed to insurance or paid out of pocket. UCSF separately lists a **$900 self-pay written review** in participating specialties. For an appointment covered through a health plan, confirm the network and coverage with the plan. [UCSF service and price comparison](https://www.ucsfhealth.org/patients-visitors/second-opinion)

For plans covered by **California Health and Safety Code §1383.15**, a second opinion about specialist care can come from a different medical group in the network, in the same or an equivalent specialty. If the network has no appropriately qualified reviewer, the plan must authorize one outside it. The plan must require the reviewer to give **both the patient and the initial clinician** the consultation report, including any tests or procedures the reviewer recommends. A plan that denies a second opinion must give the patient written reasons and explain the grievance right. Those provisions give the patient identifiable documents to request. [California law, subsections (f)–(i)](https://california.public.law/codes/health_and_safety_code_section_1383.15) · [California regulator’s codebook, §1383.15](https://www.dmhc.ca.gov/Portals/0/Docs/OLS/2025%20Knox-Keene%20Act%20and%20Title%2028%20Book/CA%20Knoxx-Keene%20Act%202025%20Edition_508r.pdf)

One way to make a review inspectable is to ask which original test, image, result or observation the next clinician examined, what still does not fit, and who will follow up. If the concern is an inaccurate entry, ask who can see the addendum when that entry is shared. Each question corresponds to a different event the Garden can document.

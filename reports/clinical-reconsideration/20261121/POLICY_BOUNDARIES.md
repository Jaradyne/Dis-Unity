## What the Garden must keep visible

A person's account of psychological harm is evidence of their reported experience. It remains visible even while clinical accuracy, causation and accountability are unresolved. Neither disagreement with a first opinion nor agreement by a second clinician settles those questions automatically.

The relevant review record should identify the question reconsidered, the material actually examined, the reasoning offered, the conclusion and its limits, and the reviewer's relationship to the original assessment. A different signature or a copied note is insufficient to establish an independent examination of the question. This is an evidence-recording standard for the Garden, not a new legal or clinical standard.

An initial opinion, a request to revisit it, an explicit refusal, a later review, an amended record, delivery of that amendment and a usable change in care are separate observations. Earlier failures and reported burdens remain part of the history after a later administrative or clinical success. Absence of a record remains UNKNOWN, not an inferred refusal.

## Contrary evidence, ordinary explanations and unknowns

- The initial opinion is explicitly provisional, not an asserted final diagnosis.
- The report of psychological burden is preserved as meaningful experience, independently of whether clinical error can be established.
- The later review agrees while retaining uncertainty; it documents consideration of primary material, not independent corroboration or proof of correctness.
- The initial assessment may be mistaken, incomplete, or reasonable under limited evidence; this fixture does not decide which.
- A reviewer can reasonably agree after examining primary material; agreement is not itself evidence that reconsideration failed.
- Service A cites capacity. The refusal is documented in the fiction; the stated reason is not independently verified.
- The accuracy of the initial and later opinions and responsibility for reported harm remain unadjudicated.
- The organizational and evidentiary independence of the later reviewer is not established.
- Whether an authored correction reached the continuing-care team, changed action or improved health is unknown.

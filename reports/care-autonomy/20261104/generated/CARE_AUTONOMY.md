# Care, Consent and State Power

When help becomes a state-directed intervention, what establishes lawful authority, meaningful choice, usable alternatives and an actual protective result?

Supplied public baseline. No individual diagnosis or eligibility determination. Formal protection is not proof that a person was protected.

## Questions about an actual offer

- What was actually offered: location, availability, duration, conditions and realistic route onward?
- Could the person use it safely, with disability access, language access, privacy and continuity of medical care?
- What would accepting it require losing: RV or possessions, partner or pets, work, transportation or trusted support?
- What reasons did the person give, how were those reasons retained, and what voluntary alternative was considered?
- What independent clinical and functional evidence supports the specific legal route, beyond homelessness or refusal?
- Who explained the choice and consequences, provided counsel or advocacy, and could correct a mistaken referral?
- What actually arrived and continued: housing, treatment, restored choice or a documented remedy?

These fields are questions for a consent-respecting public-record review; they are not a diagnosis, eligibility score or resident surveillance form.

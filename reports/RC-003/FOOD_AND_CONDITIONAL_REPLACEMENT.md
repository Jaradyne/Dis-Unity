# Food reserves and conditional replacement

Evidence cutoff: 21 September 2026. Bounded specialist work for RC-003. Findings below distinguish documented operations from conditional mechanisms. Sources and limitations are recorded in `inbox/RC-003/electric_food.json`.

## A fallback can weaken just when demand reaches it

The food-bank concern identifies a useful general test: **does the event that creates need also consume the supposed reserve?** Different organizations can still share the same farms, wholesalers, roads, fuel and funding exposure.

Food banks have several supply channels. Feeding America's FY2025 report records 4.3 billion pounds rescued out of 7.2 billion pounds sourced nationally—approximately 60%, calculated from rounded figures. This is neither a local supply ratio nor a measure of nutritional adequacy. [Feeding America FY2025 report](https://www.feedingamerica.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/FA_25AnnReport_DIGITAL_final.pdf)

Alameda County Community Food Bank's FY2025 audit explicitly distinguishes purchases, donations, government food and recovery. Its annual report describes access to Raley's vendor prices and a five-food-bank purchasing partnership supported by a $2.5 million foundation grant. These show that procurement beyond surplus already exists. They do not establish today's uncommitted inventory or emergency capacity. [ACCFB audit, issued 30 December 2025](https://www.accfb.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ACCFB-2025-Audit-FS.pdf), [ACCFB annual report](https://www.accfb.org/annual-report-2025/)

USDA also buys food competitively for nutrition assistance, including coordinated purchasing for TEFAP. Government commodities should not be treated as supermarket leftovers. Current local allocation and fulfillment remain separate questions. [USDA purchasing description](https://www.ams.usda.gov/selling-food)

The potential feedback is **production or transport loss → less usable donation supply and higher prices → more people need help → the purchasing budget buys less food**. This is a plausible mechanism, not an observed current famine. Some disruptions temporarily create more stranded surplus; an income shock can cause hunger even while shops remain stocked.

Reducing avoidable food waste is a healthy decline even when it reduces rescued donations. The transition should preserve dependable nutrition through purchasing, income and access support; it should not preserve waste to preserve the charitable supply stream.

The response therefore depends on the failure:

| What has become scarce? | Useful response | What it cannot guarantee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Household purchasing power | Benefits access, cash, predictable food purchasing | Physical supply where food itself is missing |
| Local delivery or access | Alternate routes, nearby distribution, shared transport, usable storage | Supply if all routes share the same disruption |
| Broad physical food supply | Protected rotating inventory, diversified producing regions, coordinated allocation and production support | Immediate new harvests or unlimited imports |

## A practical food floor for a commons

This is a proposal, not a claim that an organization has agreed or stocked it:

1. Define the essential meals the commons will keep providing, with community input on dietary needs and access.
2. Support that floor through predictable purchasing and a rotating stock of appropriate staples and ready-to-eat foods. Use and replenish it through normal service; do not accumulate inaccessible or expiring stock.
3. Add rescued food to that floor. Keep rescue valuable without making the minimum service depend entirely on leftovers.
4. Reserve the means of serving it: controlled storage, safe water, cooking energy, refrigeration where needed, paid core staff and accessible distribution hours.
5. Diversify geography, seasons, distributors and routes. Several suppliers buying from the same source provide less independence than their number suggests.
6. Replenish before the agreed minimum is breached; prearrange fair allocation and protect each contributing community's own floor.

Farm contracts and pooled purchasing can support growers and access together. They cannot make crops immune to shared drought or transport failure. Dry rice and beans are not a usable meal for someone without water, fuel or cooking space.

There is also a healthy-transition version of this problem: successful waste prevention can reduce rescue donations. The function to preserve is nutrition access. Creating more waste to feed the rescue channel would defeat the purpose; stable procurement and household access can grow into the opening.

## Diesel to electric: the first conditional-replacement case

The desired service is moving people, freight or equipment, and keeping food cold. Diesel combustion is one way to perform it. DOE identifies predictable return-to-base delivery, refuse and bus operations as present candidates for electric vehicles, with feasibility depending on routes, payload, terrain, weather, auxiliary loads and charging opportunity. [DOE fleet guidance](https://afdc.energy.gov/vehicles/electric-fleets)

**Replacing a vehicle and repowering its existing chassis are different projects.** DOE describes a conversion as replacing the engine with motors, batteries, high-voltage wiring and controls; packaging, weight distribution and chassis suitability require assessment. A useful old body does not establish a sound conversion. [DOE conversion guidance](https://afdc.energy.gov/vehicles/electric-conversions)

A particularly relevant staged example is older: DOE documented Portland's Meals on Wheels People using electric shore power for truck refrigeration while parked in 2018. That shed some diesel consumption while preserving the food-delivery function. It did not establish electric propulsion or today's equipment status. [DOE case study, 29 May 2018](https://afdc.energy.gov/case/3069)

The transition changes the dependency map. Repeated fuel deliveries become less important; energized electrical service, chargers, support skills and battery supply become more important. Procurement must include electrical upgrades, permitting, controls, operating costs and equipment ownership. A charger purchase alone is not a working depot. [DOE charging-infrastructure guidance](https://afdc.energy.gov/fuels/electricity-infrastructure-development)

The resilience opportunity is to electrify suitable duty first, preserve essential service through the change, and allow remaining fuel to serve harder-to-replace tasks where it remains deliverable. Loaded-route trials and an outage exercise should precede retirement of necessary capacity. Long continuous operation, remote work and high auxiliary loads require separate analysis; a local delivery success does not establish feasibility for every harvest machine or construction task.

Possible growth includes electrical installation, charger repair, qualified repowering, paid worker retraining, route aggregation and refrigeration shore power. The nutrient thread adds a second transition question: useful refinery coproducts need a replacement path as fossil throughput changes. The coordinator also tracks mineral and battery supply. Neither connection establishes that electric vehicles caused today's fertilizer stress.

## Similar expectations to test

All mechanisms in this table are conditional, not a list of observed local failures.

| Expected fallback | Why the same event might weaken it | What would make it more independent? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A library for cooling and communications | Heat increases grid load; outage or unavailable staff removes service | Protected opening rights, staff funding, passive cooling and tested critical-load power |
| Electric vehicles during diesel disruption | A concurrent feeder outage prevents recharging | Charged vehicle reserve, a genuinely separate charging path and essential-trip priority |
| Neighboring power systems or response crews | The same heat, fire or storm consumes donor reserve | Verified donor surplus, route and partners with different hazard exposure |
| Another hospital accepting diverted patients | Shared illness, utility failure or staffing shortage removes receiving capacity | Confirmed staffed beds and transport, plus protected local minimum care |
| More charity during recession | Donor income, volunteer time and nonprofit revenue can fall as need rises | Predictable funding, operating reserves and paid essential staffing |

The commons thread documents a concrete caution behind the first row: Hayward reduced library opening hours effective January 2026. That does not establish a heat-response failure, but it shows why a building's existence and usable staffed hours must be recorded separately.

For each reserve, track the essential function, protected floor, actual usable stock or service, replenishment lead time, common failure causes, access rights and an observable exercise result. Prefer independence from the relevant hazard; do not label a reserve statistically countercyclical without evidence.

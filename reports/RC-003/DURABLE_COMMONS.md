# Durable commons: control, useful capacity and credible fallback

Evidence cutoff and retrieval: 21 September 2026. Bounded public-source research; no organizations contacted, sites reserved or resources committed. Historical items below are newly researched context, not new September events.

The best next step is a small, durably controlled commons inside an existing community asset, paired with a second node whose failure conditions differ. Buying a building is one route. A funded, enforceable operating agreement can also secure the essential rights: keys, storage, opening hours, permission to host the intended activities, maintenance responsibilities and a sufficiently long term. Ordinary room reservations do not provide these rights.

## Why San Anselmo appeared

San Anselmo was an implementation example because its municipal owner confirms completed construction of a solar/battery/EV microgrid and present solar supply to Town Hall. The completion ceremony was 17 July 2026. The page describes future public charging, Wi-Fi and heating/cooling during disruptions. It does not document an exercised public activation, staffed emergency hours, battery endurance or potable-water reserve. That makes it useful for learning how a town converted an existing public asset; it does not establish the best location for an East Bay commons or spare capacity available to another community. [Town project record](https://www.sananselmo.gov/1677/Town-Hall-Resilience-Hub).

## A fallback that can shrink when needed

Hayward announced library-hour reductions effective 1 January 2026 to help close its municipal deficit. Its current schedule closes both branches on Sunday and Monday. Meetings must occur during library hours and be reserved at least 30 days ahead. A library can therefore remain a substantial physical asset while offering fewer usable public hours. This is a measured fiscal/service constraint; no heat-related service failure is established. The January notice does not establish current streaming-service status. [January notice](https://www.hayward-ca.gov/discover/news/jan26/library-hours-be-reduced-two-digital-streaming-resources-eliminated), [current hours](https://www.hayward-ca.gov/public-library/using-library/locations-hours), [reservation rules](https://www.hayward-ca.gov/public-library/using-library/meeting-rooms).

The conditional cascade is municipal revenue pressure → reduced staffed public hours → less accessible refuge/information capacity during another shock. It activates only if reduced hours overlap actual need and alternatives cannot absorb it. Extended activation staffing, protected operating funds and a separately governed partner site are buffers. Evidence of funded emergency hours and successful activation would downgrade the concern.

## A minimum useful commons to establish

Start with one accessible room plus secure storage and a functioning toilet. Daily use should include a computer/help desk, repair or skills sessions, community information and participant-led programming. Let community members govern access and priorities; ambassadors can connect nearby nodes without making one central institution responsible for everything.

Before counting a function as reserve, record its actual operating commitment:

| Function | What must be secured | Degraded service to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Space | Tenure, keys, permitted use, reliable hours, storage and fair access rules | One usable room with an accountable operator |
| Heat/cooling | Building thermal condition, measured essential electrical loads, maintenance and staffed access | A smaller protected room; switch off nonessential spaces |
| Electricity | Actual critical circuits, compatible backup, tested changeover and replenishment | Lights, communications and agreed refrigeration before discretionary charging |
| Water/sanitation | Drinking-water inventory or reliable supply, replenishment and usable sanitation | Explicit consumption and hygiene plan; no assumption that garden/rainwater is potable |
| Food | A rotating purchased staple floor, procurement funds, staff, cooking water/energy and delivery arrangements | Defined meals or packages independent of that day's rescued surplus |
| Communications | Accessible local information, offline copy and an alternate communications path | Public notices and person-to-person handoffs if the internet fails |

These are proposed design requirements, not claims that the listed nodes already satisfy them. Donated food can extend the food floor. Purchasing protects against donation withdrawal only while suppliers, routes and funding still work. Multiple donors using the same wholesaler or road do not provide independent backup.

Use two linked tracks: negotiate a multiyear operating agreement at an existing institution, while examining acquisition or land-trust stewardship for a lasting base. Protect ordinary users, paid staffing and each partner's reserve before accepting additional demand. Plan the alternate node by its electricity, water, route, staffing and funding dependencies rather than distance alone. The first proof should be a small, observed service drill with a real opening time, usable load and replenishment record—not a declaration of self-sufficiency.

# Spiritual readiness: the baseline and changes to test

Research cutoff/retrieval: **8 October 2026 UTC**. Author: bounded `military_conscience_baseline` thread, RC-005. **Staged for Jared and Digest review; not admitted to RC-004 canonical research.** Source records and access limitations: [baseline_sources.json](baseline_sources.json).

The strongest present finding is a tension that can be tested: existing doctrine protects individual conscience and self-directed assessment, while the later program-development record describes leaders assessing soldiers. Religious-affiliation coding also became less detailed. These changes warrant examining implementation and resource allocation. They do not by themselves demonstrate compelled Christianity, a religious promotion test, or a youth indoctrination program.

## The protections already on paper

**B01 — Army FM 7-22, October 2020, Change 1.** Chapter 10 treats spiritual readiness as applicable to religious and nonreligious soldiers. Sections 10-6–10-10 reject coercion and describe development as generally self-directed. Section 10-28 makes assessment self-selected and self-assessed, without repercussions or perceived negative consequences. Section 10-31 permits discussion during developmental counseling; it does not establish a promotion score. Equal-opportunity advisors appear as an available check (§10-34). This is an Army baseline, not proof of every service's practice or the current version of every implementing rule. [Official Army copy](https://home.army.mil/hood/5517/2115/1094/FM_7-22.pdf), printed pp. 10-1–10-7; assessment provision visually checked at PDF page 117.

**B02 — DoDI 1300.17, 1 September 2020.** Sections 1.2(a)–(e) protect religious observance and nonobservance. Qualified protections cover expressions of conscience, moral principle and religious belief against adverse personnel action, including denial of promotion, schooling, training or assignment. The instruction preserves readiness, discipline and safety constraints; religious accommodation denials must satisfy the stated RFRA standard when applicable. It assigns implementation responsibilities and provides an administrative appeal process. These are policy protections to audit, not a conclusion about an individual legal claim. The official endpoint returned 403; the [DMI-hosted document](https://www.dmi-ida.org/download-pdf/pdf/DoD%20Instruction%201300.17.pdf) was readable. Its cover and §1.2 were inspected; no later amendment was established in this bounded pass.

**B03 — DoDI 5120.08, 24 April 2024.** Before ORA, §2 places the Armed Forces Chaplains Board under the authority, direction and control of the Under Secretary for Personnel and Readiness. The Deputy Assistant Secretary for Military Personnel Policy, through the Assistant Secretary for Manpower and Reserve Affairs, acts on or endorses recommendations and handles the Executive Director's fitness reporting. This provides a concrete comparison for the new ORA reporting line. **Access limit:** [official PDF](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/512008p.pdf) blocked; §2 was available in the official source's search index. Full-document verification remains open.

**B04 — DoDI 1304.28, 2021, amended 8 May 2024.** Chaplain appointment and religious-organization endorsement have their own requirements, distinct from soldiers' affiliation codes. The amended instruction covers spiritual needs and permits requested counseling that is not faith-based (§3.1); it protects chaplains against compelled contrary rites (§2.2). The [amended primary-document mirror](https://www.spirit-filled.org/documents/130428p.pdf) was readable; the official endpoint was blocked. A preference-code deletion is therefore not, without a separate policy or case record, proof that a denomination lost endorsement eligibility or that its chaplains were removed.

## What Digest should test next

1. **Participation and records:** obtain the current assessment, instructions, privacy notice and data dictionary. Who can refuse, who sees individual answers, and how long are they retained? Distinguish confidential chaplain communication, self-assessment and command-maintained records.
2. **Career consequences:** seek the actual evaluation, assignment, deployment and schooling rules plus substantiated complaint outcomes. No inspected source establishes that religious affiliation or an ORA assessment currently determines promotion or clearance. Absence from this small packet is not proof of absence.
3. **Equal access after recoding:** compare access, staffing and spending for groups pooled into broad categories, including nonreligious personnel. Preserve the denominator and correction history. Administrative simplification and unequal service are competing possibilities that require results.
4. **Review and remedy:** verify current accommodation appeals, equal-opportunity and legal-review routes against actual handling times and reversals. Keep individual identities out of this public dossier unless their public official role is essential.

The useful safeguard question is whether a person can decline religious formation, retain equal access and career standing, and obtain an effective independent remedy. A formal promise helps define that test; it does not complete it.

# ICE within the larger enforcement system

**Start here for the RC-006 expansion.** This packet extends the military-conscience research into postwar history, DHS, ICE and adjacent institutions. Evidence cutoff: **10 October 2026 UTC**, with selected rechecks and the requested agency supplement on **11 October**. It is ready for Jared and Digest's discussion, not automatically admitted into the canonical model.

The evidence supports examining an interconnected enforcement system. There are identifiable personnel pathways, shared information systems, military support, detention contracts and transfers into foreign custody. Some safeguards have produced investigation and judicial intervention; other records show serious failures. These connections can be analyzed before a supposed final tipping point, without needing every soldier's private beliefs.

## What “paramilitary enforcement wing” captures—and leaves open

ICE is a civilian law-enforcement agency with armed and specialized tactical capacities. *Paramilitary* can describe military-like organization or coercive capabilities; it does not by itself identify legal authority, command or unlawful conduct. [I09] DHS is a larger department, and the Coast Guard is a genuine armed service within it. [A02–A03]

The important question is functional: **what can these institutions collectively do, to whom, on whose decision, and with what effective possibility of interruption or reversal?** A civilian designation does not provide reassurance about conduct. Nor does military experience establish an employee's religion, politics or willingness to abuse someone.

## Read the connections together

The working model links four kinds of evidence:

1. **Capacity:** people, money, equipment, information, facilities and transport that are actually available.
2. **Selection and discretion:** who becomes an enforcement target, which classifications are accepted, and who may change priorities or authorize force.
3. **Accountability:** whether affected people can be located, heard, medically assessed and represented before harm becomes irreversible.
4. **Correction:** whether a complaint, inspection or court order changes conduct and restores freedom or safety in practice.

This is a framework for comparing records, not a claim that every arrow is proved. The military's ORA authority and youth/assessment remit remain relevant to the original concern. The present collection **does not establish an ORA-directed religious recruitment or promotion program connecting to ICE**. That limits this specific linkage; it does not diminish separate evidence of coercive capacity or violations.

A predecessor is a baseline, not an acquittal of a later change. The postwar history helps identify what changed in scale, supervision, information sharing, delegated power and access to review. It also records rights expansion, repeal and successful scrutiny, so the model can recognize correction as well as deterioration.

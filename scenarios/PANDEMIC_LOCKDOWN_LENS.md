# Pandemic / Lockdown Stress Lens

This is a scenario parameter, not a forecast. It asks how a system behaves under conditions broadly analogous to different stages of COVID-19-era public-health restrictions, workforce absenteeism, disrupted travel, and recurrent supply-chain friction.

## Stages

- **P0 — preparedness / surveillance:** ordinary operations, rising disease awareness, pre-positioning and contingency planning.
- **P1 — localized outbreak:** localized absenteeism, episodic facility closures, regional healthcare pressure, voluntary behavior changes.
- **P2 — partial restrictions:** school/workplace closures, event limits, some travel restrictions, elevated delivery demand, selective quarantine/isolation.
- **P3 — broad lockdown / essential-movement regime:** sharp fall in passenger travel and discretionary activity; essential freight, food, health, utilities and public safety prioritized.
- **P4 — prolonged or recurrent emergency:** repeated waves, staff burnout, deferred maintenance, inventory attrition, supplier failure, fiscal stress and adaptation.
- **P5 — uneven reopening / recovery:** demand rebounds unevenly; bottlenecks shift from demand destruction to restocking, staffing, repair and balance-sheet damage.

## Tags to apply to systems and dependencies

A signal, reserve, route, facility or industry may be marked with one or more:
- **spared_or_demand_reduced**
- **priority_protected**
- **bends_more**
- **breaks_earlier**
- **workforce_absence_sensitive**
- **crowding_sensitive**
- **border_or_permission_sensitive**
- **maintenance_deferral_sensitive**
- **cold_chain_critical**
- **home_delivery_substitution**
- **digital_substitution**
- **recovery_backlog_sensitive**

## Medical cold chain

Pandemic conditions may raise the importance of temperature-controlled medicines, vaccines, biologics, diagnostics and laboratory materials while simultaneously stressing transport and backup power.

- CDC treats vaccine cold-chain continuity from manufacturer to administration as essential and requires emergency storage/transport planning.
- WHO notes that small refrigerated vehicles may draw refrigeration power from the vehicle engine and larger trucks/semitrailers often use independent diesel-powered refrigeration units; electrical standby may be available while parked.
- Backup generators can preserve stationary cold storage during grid outages, but create another fuel dependency. Battery/solar/passive cold storage can provide partial alternatives depending on temperature range and duration.
- A diesel crunch during P2-P4 should therefore give extra attention to reefer fuel, hospital/clinic generator fuel, cold-storage transfer capacity, dry ice/refrigerants, driver availability, and priority-fuel allocation.

## Demand is not uniform under lockdown

A lockdown can reduce some diesel demand sharply while increasing or preserving other diesel demand:
- commuting, tourism, construction and discretionary retail freight can fall;
- parcel/home delivery can rise;
- groceries, healthcare, sanitation, utilities, emergency response and cold-chain logistics remain essential;
- freight rail and long-haul trucking may have lower total traffic but higher criticality for particular commodities;
- workforce absenteeism can become the bottleneck even when fuel and equipment exist.

Every pandemic-marked stress should ask: **did demand disappear, move elsewhere, become priority-protected, or become more operationally difficult?**

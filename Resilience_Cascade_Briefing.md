# Resilience Cascade — Deployment 2

Completed **21 September 2026**, using evidence through **20 September**. This continues the first baseline; older developments found now are marked as such. The operating system and history are in [Dis-Unity](https://github.com/Jaradyne/Dis-Unity).

## WHAT MOVED

**Phosphate concern is stronger.** Newly reviewed August27 evidence from Mosaic documents actual production idles and reduced rates, attributed to sulfur availability and cost. That independently extends Nutrien's earlier signal. Farm stockouts and yield losses remain unproven. [Mosaic](https://mosaicco.com/Article/Fertilizer-Markets-Facts-and-Context).

**Fuel and transport conditions differ by region.** September11 East Coast distillate stocks fell slightly while national stocks rose; West Coast stocks rose but remained below a year earlier. USDA's September15 produce-truck reports show slight shortages across several California origins, with some shortages, alongside a slight surplus at Mexico–South Texas crossings. These do not establish diesel as the cause or missed deliveries. [EIA regional stocks](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/pdf/table6.pdf), [USDA availability data](https://agtransport.usda.gov/resource/25pi-t6xr.json), [rating definitions](https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/RTQ1stQuarter2026.pdf).

**A concrete local lifeboat candidate advanced:** San Anselmo reports its Town Hall microgrid complete and supplying solar power. Public outage operation still needs verification. [Municipal project record](https://www.sananselmo.gov/1677/Town-Hall-Resilience-Hub).

## WHAT IT TOUCHES

| Cluster | What is supported | What remains conditional |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sulfur → phosphate → farms | Named producer curtailments | Distributor shortages, late application and crop effects |
| Fuel → transport → food access | High costs, uneven stock and truck availability | Causal chain to missed essential deliveries |

BART/Muni's fiscal interpretation survived independent review: future funding-dependent service plans are not the current timetable. [BART budget](https://www.bart.gov/sites/default/files/2026-09/FY27%20Adopted%20Budget%20Memo_FINAL_SIGNED.pdf).

## WHAT CAN ABSORB IT

Harvest and grain rail/barge movements provide counterevidence to a generalized logistics stoppage. They are operating systems, but their spare capacity and suitability for refrigerated loads are unknown. [USDA grain transport](https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/GTR09172026.pdf).

Other candidates are suitable alternative suppliers, pooled deliveries, less spoilage and protected operating bridges. Capacity must be matched to the needed function: abundant potash cannot replace phosphorus, and restricted capital cannot pay operating bills.

## WHAT MAY GROW

Shared delivery coordination, nutrient-efficient agriculture, qualified repair work and staffed critical-load hubs remain practical opportunities. Preserve workers' income and restart skills during curtailments; layoffs are not automatically healthy shedding.

Watsonville's replacement imaging reached installation/licensing milestones in September. It could reduce avoidable travel if staffed regular service follows. This is healthy replacement alongside a separate operating-finance question. [District update](https://www.pvhcd.org/home-page/page/new-mri-and-ct-imaging-now-available-watsonville-community-hospital).

## LIFEBOATS

Prioritize a small accessible essential-trip pool, a verified food-access network, and a staffed room with measured backup communications. San Anselmo provides a built example; the model does not yet credit tested endurance or open-access outage hours.

## ISLANDS OF STABILITY

North American fertilizer/food production and local civic institutions remain complementary partners. CF's high available-capacity utilization excludes an idled complex, so it is not evidence of spare output. Every offer needs its own commitments, route and protected reserve check. [CF disclosure](https://ir.cfindustries.com/Investors/news/news-details/2026/CF-Industries-Holdings-Inc--Reports-First-Half-2026-Net-Earnings-of-1-34-Billion-Adjusted-EBITDA-of-2-18-Billion/default.aspx).

## COMMONS OPPORTUNITIES

The seven [proposal cards](Resilience_Cascade_Commons.md) cover shared trips, food aggregation, tools/repair, communications, nutrient efficiency, fair reserve sharing and care-access coordination. The strongest near-term move is combining compatible existing trips while protecting accessibility, driver rest and each partner's ordinary commitments. No outreach or resource commitment has been made.

## WHAT WE MAY BE WRONG ABOUT

We have not established fuel-caused delivery failures, current local water/clinical surplus, or tested public hub endurance. April EBMUD storage is a dated baseline, not September reassurance. [EBMUD report](https://www.ebmud.com/download_file/force/58167/703?WOD_-_Water_Supply_Update_-_April_14_2026_Final_Board.pdf=).

Six independent checks of first-cycle claims completed. Final peer review of these additions was interrupted; the saved admission explicitly says **coordinator acceptance with limitations**. Current social conditions, insurance and several infrastructure sectors remain incompletely covered.

## WHAT DESERVES MORE AGENTS

1. Phosphate restarts, distributor fulfillment and application windows.
2. Whether regional fuel and truck constraints become actual essential-delivery losses.
3. Tested local hub functions, accessible transport reserve and current water/care continuity.
4. Independent challenge of this cycle's additions.

# The Mirror boss — Jared's design, 25 September 2026

Status: design proposal, not a running boss or a researched claim about a service. Her name and final art are still Jared's to choose. This encounter explores **assumed care, reachable care**. Tide-Shepherd remains the keeper of attention under uncertainty; the theme does not have to belong to only one boss.

Jared's mechanic: she twirls mirrors; the player chooses which reflection to approach, shoot or avoid. A mouse-controlled shmup makes the difference between a convincing picture and a reachable route tangible.

## Recommended first playable slice

Move a small lantern-ship with the mouse. Hold the primary button to focus a test beam. Mirrors rotate through three slow lanes. Aim at a reflection to inspect the promise it projects, then decide whether to continue along that route. A focus key slows or pauses motion so reading never competes with dodging. Keyboard movement and a turn-based lane mode offer the same choices.

| Mirror response | Meaning | Visible action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The specific access claim is contradicted by the packet | For this person, route and time, the advertised path does not work | Its promise-layer cracks; the underlying source remains in the record |
| Capacity, eligibility or timing is unknown | The claim has not been resolved | Fog scatters the laser into a question-mark constellation; the mirror remains |
| The packet confirms the relevant access conditions | This particular route is usable in the stated circumstances | The beam passes through and lights a stepping-stone toward her |

Reaching her is the encounter's resolution. There is no truth score, punishment for uncertainty, or damage to people who need care. A mirror avoided is unexamined, not false. The real person behind the reflection need not be an enemy.

## Wacky moves worth trying

- **Opening Hours Waltz:** the correct doorway rotates out of reach just before arrival. Holding the focus beam reveals whether travel time fits the opening window.
- **Eligibility Prism:** one bright “available” beam splits into actual conditions. Align the route with the player's stated circumstances; do not infer a person's eligibility from appearance.
- **The Queue Behind the Glass:** a service exists but its next opening is later than the need. The route can be real and still fail this voyage.
- **The Caretaker's Reflection:** the path is open only if a driver, carer or interpreter can actually accompany it. Unknown helper capacity stays unknown.
- **Commons Ricochet:** a documented shared ride or voluntary delivery connects two otherwise disconnected platforms. A possibility is shown as a dotted beam until the arrangement is confirmed.

The first design test should use explicitly fictional fixtures: an appointment at 17:00, a doorway closing at 16:00, and a third route with unknown capacity. This isolates whether the controls communicate the distinction. Real service packets need dated public evidence and the actual relevant conditions before any “confirmed route” can appear. No prototype fixture should be presented as a live shelter, pantry, reserve or promise of help.

## What reaches the Governor

Keep the inspected/avoided mirror IDs, the questions Jared preserved, the dated packet and its original evidence labels. Any visual shattering is a reaction to a pre-existing, specific contradiction in the packet; gameplay cannot create one. Deliver after any reviewed power effects, and also when there are no powers. The Governor may select her position or finale once a playable packet exists; Jared may rearrange the series.

Tamarian-style invitation: **“The door in the mirror; the traveller at dusk.”** Plain meaning: a pictured service is not enough—we are testing whether someone can reach usable help in time.

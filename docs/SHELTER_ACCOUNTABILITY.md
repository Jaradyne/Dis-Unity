# Shelter Safety and Accountability

This wing asks whether a promised protection applies, operates and produces a remedy without repeated emergency advocacy. It uses the existing `HOMELESSNESS` Garden tree alongside the institutional and logistics work. It is a saved supplied-content pass, not an active specialist or collector.

Start with the [readable projection](../reports/shelter-accountability/generated/SHELTER.md). Its five questions cover notice/access, public oversight, service continuity, funding integrity, and patterns/remedies. The first bounded case concerns California shelter notice and review requirements. Other questions are defined, not investigated by this pass.

## The first case

The retained Civil Code §§1954.08–1954.091 text describes a defined publicly funded/overseen shelter framework. It includes other shelter sites as well as hotels/motels. Section 1954.09 conditions its non-tenancy treatment on meeting all requirements, including Housing First and procedural safeguards; the initial funding/oversight/occupancy screen does not establish all those conditions. Existing rights remain preserved.

Within that framework the ordinary rule calls for at least 30 days' written notice before proposed termination, with reasons and accommodation/grievance information. Coverage, applicable version and narrow exceptions must be examined. A qualifying direct threat differs from an asserted label; self-exit and an amended short extension have specific conditions. The exigent-paperwork provision is not a general short-notice permission. Its fallback availability deadline runs from the participant's request. Conversion provisions are flagged for specific interpretation.

The public administrator has grievance and remedy responsibilities. Follow an operator's notice through the administrator's review, any instruction and its implementation, then separately verify whether shelter access continued or resumed. Acknowledgment, referral, a decision and actual safe placement are different observations. The inspected text does not supply a universal automatic stay or guaranteed replacement bed.

Formal safeguards are documented with access/version limits. People, actual procedures, exercise of authority, observed effectiveness and particular failures remain UNKNOWN. No dated resident notice or program agreement is admitted. Short-notice concerns motivate the investigation; they do not establish a statewide rate or financial misconduct. Costs, allegations and repeated advocacy are not fraud findings or independent case counts.

## Notice rehearsals

The screen is deliberately not a legal compliance calculator or resident intake form. It accepts only the `simulated` mode, `SIM-` case IDs and `FICT-` record IDs. It rejects malformed/reversed dates, extra fields and unsupported input types. Calendar subtraction has no authority to decide legal service or counting rules.

Unknown coverage, versions, conversion status, service dates and exceptions survive as review flags. An asserted direct threat, self-exit or short extension routes to evidence review. Exigent paperwork never silently waives the ordinary interval screen. Missing notice content remains visible even with a long interval. Every output keeps legal finding NOT DETERMINED, fraud NOT ASSESSED and protective outcome UNKNOWN, including the 30-day example.

## History and evidence limits

This is late discovery of older legal material, not a newly enacted 30-day rule or an observed shelter incident. Current official code retrieval was blocked; consistent statutory mirrors and official historical legislative analysis are retained. The latest inspected version note is January 1, 2026, not the rule's origin. Absence of later amendments has not been exhaustively verified. The [legal review](../reports/shelter-accountability/LEGAL_REVIEW.md) records the precise scope, dates, exceptions and primary-source access gaps.

Retained web-tool responses are complete as returned by that tool, saved after retrieval with a save timestamp and retrieval date. They are not raw origin-server HTTP bodies/headers, and do not establish an exact origin retrieval instant. No new HTTP adapter is introduced. Restricted source text is not claimed read. No private resident record or identifying complaint is included.

Future evidence must have its own source identity, dates, locators and retained material. Review it before adding an attributed assessment revision. Keep earlier evidence and contrary assessments; corrections are appended. A particular program or resident case requires its own scope and IDs, not relabelling this formal baseline as an incident. Input pins and generated output are updated only as part of a reviewable change; a hash verifies identity, not truth or legal sufficiency.

RC-007 is review intake, with its required canonical baseline snapshot. RC-004 remains canonical; RC-005/RC-006, the Camp case and all earlier saved passes remain unchanged. No Governor powers, model calls, Bees, schedules, canonical admission, complaint or FOIA submission are enabled.

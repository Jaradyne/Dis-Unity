# Camp East Montana / Fort Bliss: one Garden case

**Question:** What safeguards existed, which failed in documented practice, and what evidence shows whether those failures were corrected?

**Result:** The retained GAO audit documents failures of the pre-occupancy inspection, detention standards and contract oversight. It records some remedial steps, but does not verify that the cited safety failures were corrected after the contractor transition. All four GAO recommendations remained Open on the October 12 product-page recheck. That status is not proof that no corrective work occurred.

## Evidence standard

A verified correction needs a named deficiency and requirement, a completed action, its date, and evidence that the action actually satisfied the requirement. An agency promise, contract award, overall inspection rating or recommendation status alone does not complete that chain. The same standard applies regardless of agency or contractor.

The primary audit is **RC-006 C01 / GAO-26-108886**, chiefly its retained [PDF](../../../digestion/threshold/research/2026-10-10-postwar-ice/retrievals/gao-public-documents/api/0001.pdf). Locators below are the report's **printed page numbers**, not PDF viewer numbers. Pages 25–30 are scanned agency letters and were visually read; the acquisition diagram on page 8 was also visually read. GAO's underlying contract, ICE inspection and correspondence records were not independently retrieved for this case. No source names a particular person's private religious beliefs or establishes a hidden motive.

Parent events **IC02** (opening) and **IC03** (administration transfer) are preserved unchanged. Case events CEM01–CEM22 expand their detail without inserting new events into RC-006. The PDF's original HTTP capture was October 10; catalog review was October 11; this case read and the [product-page status recheck](status-recheck.json) were October 12. Publication was June 9, 2026. These are distinct dates. The recheck supplements the case and does not alter RC-006's cutoff.

## Authority and responsibility

The reported chain is DHS's February 12, 2025 assistance request → Army procurement on ICE's behalf → ICE administration from October 1, 2025. After two failed ICE contracting attempts, Army and ICE jointly chose Army procurement, with ICE providing operational support and leading oversight (pp2,7). The Army solicited on June 13 and awarded the task order July 18 using the Navy-established **WEXMAC** small-business vehicle. The maximum value was **up to $1.3 billion**, not an amount shown as spent (pp7–10).

ICE ERO approved the contractor's performance work statement; ICE contracting officials did not review it before award. The Army used lowest-price-technically-acceptable selection and incorporated the statement without revision. GAO reports that the prime lacked detention experience; several proposed subcontractors had relevant experience. The Army exercised an option year, then a September 23 modification transferred administration effective October 1 (pp2,7,9–10). ICE's operational and oversight responsibilities preceded that transfer.

This traces the documented contracting mechanism and allocation of responsibilities. The full assistance/delegation instrument was not examined; this case does not pronounce on the legal validity of every procurement or detention action. Fort Bliss location does not make every action an Army action.

| Proposed revised capacity/pricing | DHS May 29 letter planned 4,000 beds, initially funding 1,500 and adding 250-bed increments (p27) | An executed modification or actual reduction |

ICE cited separation by gender/risk and medical isolation to explain using all five units. GAO observed subdivisions within units. DHS also described surge capacity and short-stay staging near an air hub (pp25–26). Those are relevant explanations, not measurements of necessary reserve. The bounded record cannot establish current safely usable capacity.

## Safeguards, failures and correction evidence

| Safeguard and responsible actor | Documented failure | What subsequent evidence establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICE Custody Management Division pre-occupancy inspection under February 8, 2022 policy | No inspection before occupancy; officials cited expedited opening. GAO found no effective control preventing occupancy without inspection/standards (pp15–16). | Officials reported an informal post-occupancy inspection. DHS asserted existing controls and sought closure; GAO disagreed. No completed new occupancy gate demonstrated. |
| Contract incorporation of 2025 National Detention Standards; contractor quality assurance and daily supervision | Cameras/blind spots and monitor staffing, accessible showers, recreation, legal/family visitation and classification gaps at opening/September visit (pp14–15). | One recreation yard opened but was inadequate on September 16. Table 1 explicitly does not evaluate fixes after September. No item-level subsequent compliance certification in this record. |
| Medical standards and ICE Health Services Corps oversight | TB testing substituted with questionnaires, untreated chronic-care needs, missing comprehensive assessments (p17); February ODO inspection found TB-notification failure (p16 n37). | Oversight detected deficiencies. No dated post-transition medical verification in this case. |
| Weapons, movement, sanitation, use-of-force/death reporting and suicide precautions | Loaded firearm loss; escape; sanitation failures; missing reports/evidence after a death classified as homicide; suicide observation and room-design failures (pp17–18). | Eight discrepancy reports plus one medical oversight report. Some reports remained draft/pending in March. Original contractor described ongoing/planned actions; actual completion and effectiveness are unverified. |
| Contract performance surveillance and remedies | Army did not finalize/include a Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan. ICE officials said absent plan/metrics prevented financial penalties (pp18–19). | DHS later described generic penalty powers and reports under both vendors (p27). It did not provide evidence here of a penalty imposed, an implemented surveillance plan, or effective closure of the specific gaps. |
| Acquisition cost control and lessons | Full services paid before occupancy and no occupancy-sensitive pricing; GAO quantified waste (pp11–14). | Replacement award March 12 and April termination/transition documented; outcome too early to assess. May tiered-pricing and assessment promises; August Army milestones. No realized savings or complete remedy verified by this record. |

The February 2026 ODO inspection rated the facility **acceptable while finding 49 deficiencies, including three priority issues** (p16 n37). These facts coexist. The rating does not prove all deficiencies were corrected. GAO did not audit every detention standard. The January deaths and March reporting dates must not be conflated; a coroner's homicide classification is not a criminal conviction.

Defense's May 22 response agreed to assess lessons but disputed GAO's conclusions and balance, citing its own earlier documentation and rebuttal. GAO maintained that both agencies made consequential decisions (pp22,29–30). That underlying rebuttal was not independently read. DHS's May 29 response agreed with its recommendations, argued existing inspection policy was adequate, and described planned contract changes. The competing positions are retained, rather than silently resolved by agency label.

## Later retained GAO follow-up

**C02 / GAO-26-108663**, published September 24, 2026, supplies a later dated observation: as of **August 2026**, the replacement contract still lacked the cost-saving measures and ICE continued to pay for services it did not need (pp7–8); contract prices were under negotiation (p4 n14). It also states the February assistance agreement remained active as of August (p7 n25). This is evidence of pricing nonimplementation at that date, not an inference from Open status. It does not establish October pricing or medical/safety correction. C02 explicitly cites C01; repeated findings are not new corroboration. Its retained [PDF](../../../digestion/threshold/research/2026-10-10-postwar-ice/retrievals/gao-public-documents/api/0002.pdf) and HTTP record are pinned alongside C01. The scoped case event CEM22 links to parent IC07.

## Correction status and residual

**Verified in the inspected GAO record:** a recreation yard opened (not enough to meet the documented need); post-occupancy oversight occurred; a replacement contract was awarded and the original terminated; the Army supplied planning milestones to GAO. These are concrete actions or partial improvements. **Not verified:** effective correction of the enumerated safety failures or completed pricing/control reforms.

The October 12 page displays four Open recommendations: tiered pricing (DHS target end-2026), new inspection controls (DHS relies on existing policy; GAO rejects sufficiency), Army lessons paper (August milestones, December 30 target), and DHS lessons assessment (planned; completion not shown). Future targets are not overdue as of this case date. The same GAO page and PDF do not constitute independent corroboration.

**One proposed follow-up:** obtain one dated official post-April 18 medical reinspection/closure record testing the TB screening and notification deficiencies. See [the bounded Question](follow-up-question.json). No Bee is created and no retrieval is scheduled. Broader unresolved issues remain visible without creating a queue of additional tasks.

# Caretaker Reality Check — first principle for pressure points

## Root question

Whenever a system appears to rely on the assumption that **"someone will take care of it,"** ask:

> **Who, exactly? With what authority, funding, capacity, trigger, route, and deadline?**

Do not accept an abstract institution as an operational answer.

"Government," "the market," "charity," "the hospital," "the carrier," "insurance," "a bank," "mutual aid," or "someone will lend them money" are placeholders until a real mechanism is identified.

## Reality fields

For any critical function, record:

- **function:** what must continue;
- **named caretaker(s):** actual organization/role if known;
- **legal/contractual duty:** what is truly required vs customary/assumed;
- **activation trigger:** what makes the response start;
- **authority:** what the caretaker may actually do;
- **funding/cashflow:** money available now vs reimbursement later;
- **capacity:** staff/equipment/vehicles/stock actually available;
- **access:** can they reach the affected place/function;
- **time-to-response:** hours/days/weeks, not "eventually";
- **dependencies:** what the caretaker itself relies on;
- **backup caretaker:** who takes over if the first cannot;
- **tomorrow test:** what happens if the need appears tomorrow morning;
- **evidence status:** operating proof / plan-on-paper / informal expectation / unknown.

## The paper trap

A plan can be real and still fail operationally.

Distinguish:
- authority exists on paper;
- funding is appropriated;
- procurement can occur;
- contractor/vendor exists;
- dispatch can occur;
- route remains passable;
- actual service reaches the endpoint.

Each is a separate edge.

## Pressure-point rule

"Someone will take care of it" becomes a high-interest pressure point when:
1. the function has low tolerance for interruption;
2. responsibility is diffuse or conditional;
3. the apparent caretaker also depends on the stressed system;
4. reimbursement/financing lags the moment of need;
5. ordinary discourse assumes continuity without naming the mechanism.

## Hopeful redirect

If the caretaker is missing, do not stop at "nobody."

Ask:
- who already performs an adjacent function;
- whether demand can be bundled;
- whether a reciprocal route can make service viable;
- whether a temporary public contract/procurement mechanism exists;
- whether the function can be decomposed into smaller local pieces;
- whether another mode/operator can inherit the route/function.

Preserve the function, not necessarily the institution.

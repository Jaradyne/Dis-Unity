## Why retroactive work is necessary

A thousand individually explainable changes can be information-poor if each is evaluated in isolation. The missing quantity is often **trajectory**:

- what the office/function looked like before;
- when authority/reporting lines changed;
- when personnel criteria changed;
- when legal/oversight friction changed;
- when doctrine/language changed;
- when resources/capabilities moved;
- whether changes persisted, were reversed, or accumulated in the same organizational network.

Without that timeline, "nothing unusual in this one event" can accidentally erase the pattern we are trying to test. Equally, retroactive baselines protect against false alarms by showing that an apparently alarming practice is longstanding or normal.

Record why each deeper historical pull was needed. Avoid an unbounded archive crawl.

## Required event model

Every change needs enough structure for later longitudinal comparison:

- event / effective / retrieval dates;
- institution / office / command;
- function affected;
- previous state;
- new state;
- authority/source establishing the change;
- implementation status;
- personnel predecessor/successor when relevant;
- resource/capability change when relevant;
- legal/oversight check before and after;
- ordinary explanation / control;
- countervailing action or reversal;
- information lineage;
- uncertainty;
- next discriminating test.

Do not force a political interpretation field.

## Fine-grain ORA rule

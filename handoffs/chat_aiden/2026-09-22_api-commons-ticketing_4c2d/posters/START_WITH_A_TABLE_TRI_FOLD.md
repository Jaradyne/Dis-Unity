# You can begin with a table

**Tri-fold brochure source — proposed poster/handout**

## Outside panel — front cover

# YOU CAN BEGIN WITH A TABLE.

Shade.  
Some tools.  
Food.  
And people who like being there.

**A commons does not have to begin as an institution.  
It can begin as a place people are allowed to make useful together.**

---

## Outside panel — fold-in flap

### START WITH WHAT YOU CAN ACTUALLY HOLD

A table you can use.  
A patch of shade.  
A yard, room, sidewalk-adjacent space, courtyard, park meetup, garage, or mobile setup where gathering is permitted.

Bring one useful thing:

- a repair kit
- a pot of food
- a water jug
- art supplies
- a charger
- seeds or planters
- a folding chair
- a skill you can show someone else

You do **not** need to provide everything.

---

## Outside panel — back

### LEAVE IT MORE CAPABLE THAN YOU FOUND IT

Ask:

**What does this place need?**  
**What can it offer?**  
**What keeps it useful when one person is gone?**  
**What can travel back on the return trip?**  
**What still works if the power or internet goes out?**

Write down what worked.

Teach somebody else.

When the table becomes crowded, don't only make a bigger table.

**Help another table appear.**

---

## Inside panel 1

### PEOPLE FIRST

A commons is not successful because it has impressive equipment.

Look for ordinary signs of life:

People linger.  
People return.  
People make things.  
People teach.  
People rest.  
People change the space.  
People bring something of themselves.

Beauty and joy are useful infrastructure too.

---

## Inside panel 2

### LET CAPABILITY ACCUMULATE

One table can become many different things:

**Repair** — tools, fixing, making  
**Food** — meals, gardens, compost, preservation  
**Learning** — workshops, notes, apprenticeships  
**Comfort** — shade, warmth, cooling, seating  
**Power** — charging, lights, refrigeration  
**Connection** — internet, radios, bulletin boards  
**Mobility** — shared rides, deliveries, return loads  
**Culture** — music, art, stories, celebration

No single place needs every strand.

---

## Inside panel 3

### CONNECT WITHOUT BECOMING DEPENDENT

Maybe one place has tools.

Another has compost.

Another has a freezer.

Another has solar power at noon.

Another has a van.

Share what can safely be spared.

Protect the people already relying on each place.

Keep a fallback.

**A network gets stronger when every node can receive help — and eventually offer some kind of help back.**

---

### Footer

**Start useful. Stay modifiable. Teach what you learn.  
Let the commons grow because people use it.**

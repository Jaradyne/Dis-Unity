# Parallax language expansion roster — 25 September 2026

Status: **design/research roster, not a claim that one source exists in every language.**

## Keep two lanes separate

### Lane A — equal-authenticity / official-surface Parallax
For the first WHO Pandemic Agreement encounter, WHO itself supplies six equally authentic texts:
- Arabic
- Chinese
- English
- French
- Russian
- Spanish

Only these belong in the equal-authenticity comparison unless another institution publishes an authoritative rendering of the same instrument.

### Lane B — wider language garden
Jared wants a much wider set of language surfaces available for later bosses and experiments. A lane may be used only when we have one of:
1. an official/local-government rendering of the same material;
2. a clearly attributed institutional translation;
3. an explicitly synthetic translation experiment, labelled **non-evidence**.

Do not silently mix these provenance classes.

## Requested language families / country surfaces

- **Persian / Farsi** — Persian (فارسی). Iran is the primary state-language context.
- **Hebrew** — עברית.
- **Singapore** — English, Malay, Mandarin Chinese and Tamil are official; Malay is the national language. Use all four when the same official Singapore material exists.
- **Indonesia** — Bahasa Indonesia / Indonesian.
- **Philippines** — Filipino and English are official for communication/instruction; regional Philippine languages can form local surfaces when source material exists.
- **Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC)** — French is official; Lingala, Kikongo, Kiswahili and Tshiluba have national-language status.
- **Republic of the Congo (Congo-Brazzaville)** — French is official; Lingala and Kituba are major national/vehicular languages.
- **Ethiopia** — never collapse this into one “Ethiopian” language. Useful federal/public surfaces include Amharic, Afaan Oromo, Tigrinya, Somali and Afar; source status must be attached to the actual issuing institution.
- **Eritrea** — useful public/administrative surfaces include Tigrinya, Arabic and English. Legal descriptions of “official” status vary by source; record the source rather than flattening that ambiguity.
- **German**
- **Swedish**
- **Finnish**
- **Polish**
- **Irish / Gaeilge**

## Additional requested/obvious crosslinks

- Singapore adds **Malay** and **Tamil** beyond the six WHO languages; Mandarin is already represented by Chinese but should preserve Singapore-specific terminology when comparing Singapore sources.
- Philippines can add **Filipino**; do not silently call Filipino and Tagalog identical in all formal contexts.
- DRC and Congo-Brazzaville both provide **Lingala** surfaces, which creates an interesting same-language/different-state comparison opportunity.
- Ethiopia and Eritrea both provide **Tigrinya** surfaces, another possible same-language/different-institution comparison.
- Indonesian and Malay are closely related but must remain separate language standards in provenance.

## Boss rule

A language being interesting is not enough to manufacture a comparison.

Every flotsam item must retain:
- source institution;
- exact language/variant;
- source date/version;
- whether the text is authentic, official translation, institutional translation, or synthetic experiment;
- the original text span;
- any gloss used for Jared;
- ordinary translation explanation;
- whether the difference survived the Noise Gate.

The wider language roster is a **garden of possible surfaces**, not a demand to crawl all languages every cycle.

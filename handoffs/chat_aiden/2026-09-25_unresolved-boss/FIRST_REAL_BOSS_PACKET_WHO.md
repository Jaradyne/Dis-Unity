## Source family

**WHO Pandemic Agreement, WHA78.1, adopted 20 May 2025.**

WHO Article 35 states that the Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian and Spanish texts are **equally authentic**. This makes the agreement a useful Parallax control surface: the institution itself expects semantic uniformity across all six texts.

## Boring control

### Primary responsibility

English assigns States **primary responsibility** for the health and well-being of their peoples.

French says, in effect, that health and well-being fall **first and foremost** to States.

Spanish says States are the **principal responsible parties**.

**Disposition: routine.** The grammar moves; the responsibility assignment does not appear to.

## Three surviving flotsam pieces

### 1. Accountability / responsabilité / rendición de cuentas

Article 3.5 pairs inclusivity and transparency with:
- English: **accountability**
- French: **responsabilité**
- Spanish: **rendición de cuentas**

**Ordinary explanation:** French institutional/legal usage often uses `responsabilité` where English uses accountability. This can be terminology, not policy divergence.

**Why preserve it:** Spanish explicitly evokes giving an account, while French uses the broader responsibility word. That is a real human-facing semantic asymmetry worth looking at without inferring motive or legal difference.

### 2. Who is “together”?

Chapter II:
- English: **The world together equitably**
- French: **Communauté internationale – ensemble en toute équité**
- Spanish: **El mundo unido con equidad**

**Ordinary explanation:** headings are often naturalized rather than translated literally.

**Why preserve it:** French names **the international community** as the actor, while English and Spanish frame **the world**. That may be ordinary editorial adaptation, but it changes the imagined subject enough to deserve one calm second look.

### 3. What does “care workforce” encompass?

Article 7:
- English: **Health and care workforce**
- French: **Personnels de santé et d’aide à la personne**
- Spanish: **Trabajadores de la salud y asistenciales**

**Ordinary explanation:** health/social-care occupations have different conventional category labels across languages.

**Why preserve it:** French explicitly names personal assistance; Spanish uses a care/support category; English compresses both into “care.” Before interpreting significance, compare WHO terminology/glossary use.

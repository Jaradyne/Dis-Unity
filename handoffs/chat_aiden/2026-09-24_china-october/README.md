## What is established

- China's 2026 export-license catalogue requires quota documentation for the relevant refined-product export licenses. This establishes a permission stage, not a shipment or October volume. [MOFCOM/GACC, issued 30 December 2025; published 31 December](https://wms.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/wmgl/art/2025/art_c7cfa341670b456c8119042be393e062.html).
- The 2026 truck-replacement policy gives electric trucks priority. Its Chinese notice also addresses unresolved 2025 subsidy funding and prohibits using 2026 central subsidy allocations to fill earlier gaps. This creates a concrete cash-flow question; it does not establish a current unpaid balance in a particular province. [MOT/NDRC, signed 20 March; published 28 April 2026](https://xxgk.mot.gov.cn/jigou/zhghs/202604/t20260427_4204392.html).
- The heavy-truck plan sets 2030 targets of over 1.6 million new-energy heavy trucks, roughly 20% of the fleet, and about 3,000 charging/swapping stations. It also supports grid upgrades and repair access. These are targets, and the new-energy category is broader than battery-only trucks. [MOT and ten other bodies, signed 29 May; published 12 June 2026](https://xxgk.mot.gov.cn/jigou/zhghs/202606/t20260612_4207423.html).
- Counterevidence to a simple worsening story: a Reuters report on 18 September attributes an August diesel-export increase to customs data. The underlying customs series was not retrieved, so this remains an attributed lead for verification rather than an independently checked measurement. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/chinas-august-refined-fuel-exports-exceed-pre-iran-war-levels-jet-fuel-exports-2026-09-18/).

The older policies are newly inspected background, not new September announcements. The direct customs-page retrieval timed out. A search failure is not proof that an allocation was withheld.

## October observations that would change the answer

| Stage | Record to seek | What must stay separate |
|---|---|---|
| Permission | MOFCOM allocation, amendments, recipient scope and validity | Annual/batch quota; monthly operating authorization; diesel versus combined products |
| Production and domestic cover | Refinery throughput, product yields, maintenance and dated usable stocks | A profitable export opportunity versus fuel available after domestic commitments |
| Movement | Product-specific customs exports and dated loadings/arrivals | Clearance, departure and arrival; destination and fuel grade |
| Importer resilience | One receiving region's accessible stocks, demand and confirmed next cargo | National inventory versus the fuel a named service can receive |
| Electrification | Paid subsidies, delivered/registered trucks, energized chargers and completed freight work | Targets, sales, working fleet and diesel actually displaced |

For an apparent official discrepancy, first test units, calendars, preliminary revisions, customs categories, republication dates and summary length. Preserve both originals. Escalate only a remaining difference that changes the operational meaning.

## Conditional knock-on effects

**If** export permission or domestic availability tightens **and** replacement arrivals cannot bridge importer stock cover, Asian buyers may bid for other cargoes. Longer routes and working-capital needs could then affect freight, cold-chain food and essential deliveries. Confirmation requires missed or reduced cargoes and receiving-market stocks/service data; higher prices alone are insufficient. Timely replacement cargoes and maintained service would weaken the proposed link.

**If** electric trucks reliably displace diesel work, local delivery may become less exposed to liquid-fuel shocks. This does not guarantee additional Chinese exports: refiners can change production, domestic stocking or product yields, and permissions still apply. Compare freight work and actual fuel use, not just vehicle counts. A dip in diesel demand can also reflect weaker activity, efficiency, rail substitution or other fuels.

Electrification changes dependencies: electricity, chargers, batteries, maintenance, financing and repair access become part of the continuity test. The present documents establish a policy push; they do not establish how much October service can be carried by it.

## Options and limits

- **Buffer:** dated compatible stock and confirmed deliveries; usable quantities and reserve floors remain unknown.
- **Safe shedding:** combine trips and use return loads where delivery windows allow. Protect food temperatures, accessibility, essential visits and driver rest.
- **Adaptation and growth:** begin with a suitable depot-return route; verify payload, charging time, grid access, repairs and payment terms before expanding. Shared charging and battery/vehicle service can become new local capabilities.
- **Lifeboat:** a confirmed substitute carrier, appropriate rail service or an already-operating electric vehicle on the affected route. No such spare capacity is established here.
- **Outside support:** export permission, donor stock after commitments, compatible product, transport and receiving access must all be verified. Unknown surplus remains unknown.
- **Commons option:** an opt-in shared route and backhaul board for food/community deliveries, recording vehicle availability, fuel/charging needs and fallback contacts without publishing recipient details. No partner or resource is committed.

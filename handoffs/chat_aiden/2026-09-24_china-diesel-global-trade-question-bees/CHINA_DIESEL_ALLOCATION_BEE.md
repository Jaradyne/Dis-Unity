# China Diesel Allocation Bee

## Core distinction

Do not merge these into one number:

1. **formal export-control / licensing rules** — what official Chinese publications say may be exported and under what licensing class;
2. **allocation / quota rounds** — company/product allowances reported by official notices where public, otherwise clearly attributed trade reporting;
3. **actual customs exports** — what physically cleared customs by product/month/destination when available;
4. **refinery marketing plans** — announced or source-reported intended cargoes;
5. **domestic reserve / demand policy** — why authorities may tighten or relax export controls;
6. **real maritime flow** — vessel/port movement, which may lag or diverge from allocations.

## Immediate 2026 watch

Current public evidence indicates:
- refined oil products remain subject to export licensing in China's 2026 export-license catalogue;
- trade reporting says Beijing tightened refined-product exports in March 2026 to protect domestic supply during Middle East disruption, then relaxed controls later;
- Reuters reported September gasoline/diesel/jet exports expected just above 4 million tonnes, with diesel above 1 million tonnes;
- Reuters reported PetroChina, Sinopec and Zhejiang Petrochemical received most of those allowances, and a third quota/allocation round was anticipated;
- Reuters, using customs data, reported August diesel exports of about 1.33 million tonnes, the highest since March 2024.

Treat the Ministry of Commerce 20-million-tonne 2026 fuel-oil **import** allowance as a separate mechanism; it is not the diesel export quota.

## Bee lanes

### Official Surface Leaf
Watch native Chinese MOFCOM, customs, NDRC and State Council surfaces for:
- export-license catalogue changes;
- quota/allocation notices;
- emergency supply language;
- refinery/export policy;
- product-specific restrictions.

### English/translation Parallax Leaf
Watch official English counterpart wording separately.

### Allocation Ledger
For each reported allocation:
- date announced/reported;
- round;
- product;
- tonnes;
- recipient company;
- source type: official / company / trade-source reporting;
- whether quota, license, allowance, plan or actual export;
- expiry/use window;
- confidence.

### Customs Flow Leaf
Track monthly:
- diesel/gasoil;
- gasoline;
- jet/kerosene;
- marine/fuel oil;
- destinations where public/free data permit.

### Maritime Reality Leaf
Use free port/AIS aggregates to ask whether physical departures are directionally consistent with the allocation/customs picture. AIS does not by itself prove cargo content.

## Questions worth birthing

- Did the next Chinese refined-product allocation round actually appear, and where?
- How much of each allocation translated into customs-cleared diesel?
- Which destination regions absorbed the change?
- Did domestic diesel demand/price/refinery utilization move in the opposite direction?
- Are Chinese exports replacing lost Russian/Middle Eastern barrels or merely changing trade geography?
- Are shipping/freight constraints preventing nominal allocation from becoming usable delivered supply?

## Discrepancy rule

A mismatch is a Question, not an accusation.

Possible explanations include timing, customs classification, bonded/bunker trade, re-export, refinery scheduling, quota carried forward, reporting lag, commercial cancellation, destination transshipment or source error.

## Electric coastal freight: two ships delivered, service proof still missing

**Q-RESEARCH-Q18 · epoch 1 · provisional, awaiting Work review**

China's electric-freight push has crossed one important boundary: the [China Classification Society](https://www.ccs.org.cn/ccswz/articleDetail?id=202607060127336064) reports delivery of a second 740-TEU pure-electric coastal container ship. Its charge-plus-swap system and propulsion redundancy are concrete engineering buffers, not merely targets.

What is not yet established is the function Jared actually cares about: reliable completed freight. This bounded pass found no primary public record of loaded voyages, timetable completion, charging uptime, maintenance downtime or diesel actually displaced. The first ship's [CCS certification page](https://www.ccs.org.cn/ccswz/articleDetail?id=202604070331469396) was visible only through its official search excerpt; direct retrieval timed out.

**Small sample:** `顺利交付` means “successfully delivered.” It opens the door to service; it does not mean the ship has already performed scheduled service reliably. Ordinary control: the same is true of a newly delivered diesel ship or train.

**Next test:** one dated operator or port route card with loaded cargo, planned/actual departures and arrivals, energy replenishment, interruptions and fallback use. A practical commons option is an opt-in reliability board for last-verified route performance and backup capacity, without customer or crew details.

**Reflection:** replication matters. It moves the evidence from aspiration to physical capacity. But delivery, revenue entry, completed freight and dependable diesel displacement are four different rungs.
